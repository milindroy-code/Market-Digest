Explainer: Negative Free Cash Flow — Jab Ek Company Apni Kamayi Se Zyada Kharch Karti Hai

Free cash flow (FCF) woh paisa hai jo ek company apne operations se kamane ke baad bachta hai, uske capital expenditure (naye assets, factories, data-centers) mein invest karne ke baad. Jab yeh number negative ho jaata hai, matlab company apni operating earnings se zyada capex kar rahi hai — woh gap debt ya cash reserves se fund karna padta hai. Kal raat Meta ne apne pichle ek dashak mein pehli baar negative FCF report kiya, seedha apne massive AI data-center aur chip-buying spending ki wajah se. Investors isse isliye seriously lete hain kyunki yeh ek direct signal hai ki AI-infrastructure build-out ab itna expensive ho chuka hai ki sabse profitable tech companies bhi apni operating cash se usse fund nahi kar pa rahi — unhe borrow karna pad raha hai ya reserves use karne pad rahe hain. Agar yeh trend continue hota hai, toh sawal uthta hai ki is spending se utna hi revenue-growth aayega bhi ya nahi, jo poore "AI trade" ki valuation-thesis ka core hai. Yahi doubt hai jisne Nasdaq, SOX (chip-makers ka index) aur phir Asia tak ek chain-reaction trigger kiya — ek US company ka balance-sheet disclosure, jo duniya bhar ke chip-adjacent stocks ko hila gaya.

Risk-Reward Ratio

Kisi bhi trade mein enter karne se pehle, jo aap lose kar sakte hain (stop-loss tak ki distance) use jo aap gain kar sakte hain (target tak ki distance) se compare karo. Ek 1:3 risk-reward ratio wala trade overall profitable reh sakta hai chahe aap zyada baar galat hi kyun na ho — yahi wajah hai professional traders sirf win-rate nahi, balki expectancy ki baat karte hain. Aaj jaisa divergent-signal din — GIFT Nifty ka gap-up lekin overnight global weakness ke saath — discipline aur pehle-se-tayyar risk-reward framework ka importance aur badha deta hai. Yeh ek educational concept hai, kisi specific trade ki recommendation nahi.
