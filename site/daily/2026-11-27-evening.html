Explainer: Ek Hormuz Scare Rupee Aur Inflation Ke Through Market Ko Kaise Hit Karta Hai

India apni crude requirement ka ~85% import karta hai, aur historically usme se 40-50% Strait of Hormuz se guzarta tha (ek government statement ke mutabiq, ab is saal ~70% imports Hormuz ke bahar se route ho rahe hain, pehle ke ~55% se upar). Jab bhi Hormuz mein tanker-attacks jaisi reports aati hain, crude turant upar jaata hai — lekin asli hit seedha oil companies par nahi, balki ek chain reaction ke through aata hai: costlier crude → bada import bill → dollar-demand badhti hai → rupee kamzor hota hai (aaj ₹92.98/$ tak) → imported inflation ka dar badhta hai. Ek 10% crude-price rise se India ki CPI inflation ~30 basis points tak badh sakti hai, RBI data cite karte hue ek analysis ke mutabiq. Isi wajah se aisi din auto aur rate-sensitive sectors (jo consumer-spending par depend karte hain) sabse zyada hit hote hain — jabki aaj ka IT selloff khud crude se linked nahi tha, balki ek separate profit-booking story thi.

Max Pain Theory

Yeh idea hai ki ek underlying expiry tak us strike price ki taraf gravitate karta hai jahan option buyers, collectively, sabse zyada paisa lose karte hain — kyunki option sellers (aksar bade, better-capitalized players) us outcome se benefit karte hain. Yeh ek widely-watched lekin imperfect signal hai, ek guaranteed prediction ke bajaye ek input ki tarah useful. Sequential rotation se aaj ka pick, "concept" tag ke saath — aur aaj ki Technicals section mein jo gap discuss hua (fresh PCR/max-pain reading na milna), usse directly connect karta hai: jab fresh options-positioning data missing ho, max pain jaise concepts yaad dilate hain ki options market apna hi ek gravitational pull rakhta hai, jo headline index-moves se alag chal sakta hai. Yeh sirf ek educational concept hai, kisi specific trade ki recommendation nahi.
