A Fed rate hike is now priced as a near-certainty — not the cut markets would normally expect this late in a cycle. That inversion is the story: oil-driven inflation is forcing the Fed's hand even as growth cools, and the decision lands at 11:30pm IST tonight. Nifty already sits at a five-month low after Tuesday's reversal, and Indian bond yields have risen for a fourth straight week as the same crude shock works through rates here too.

Global Cues

The Fed decision is tonight's main event — and the setup has shifted. A hawkish Fed hike is now seen as a near-certainty, driven less by a strong US economy than by a hot inflation print following the oil shock. The decision and updated dot plot land at 2:00pm ET (11:30pm IST), with a press conference to follow. US markets fell Tuesday heading in: the Dow lost 328 points (−0.63%) to 52,093.11, the S&P 500 slid 0.45% to 7,585.73, and the Nasdaq dropped 0.78% to 25,981.57, as the 10-year Treasury yield surged to a multi-year high.

Brent crude is at $108.48/barrel (+2.6%), WTI above $102. Saudi Aramco has suspended the East–West pipeline entirely pending a damage assessment, with no timeline given — roughly 4 million barrels a day (about 4% of global supply) is in limbo. Gold is at $4,320.80/oz, up $28 on safe-haven flows. The rupee fell more than 1% last week to ₹95.55/$ and stays under pressure into tonight's decision.

Explainer

Why a Fed hike caused by oil is worse for India than one caused by growth. Normally, the Fed raises rates because the US economy is running hot — which usually means global demand is strong too, a backdrop India can grow into. This time is different: the hike being priced in tonight is a response to an inflation spike from the Saudi pipeline outage, not strong growth. That combination — higher US rates and a global supply shock — squeezes India from both sides. Costlier oil widens India's import bill and current account deficit (it imports ~90% of its crude), while a hawkish Fed pulls foreign capital toward safer, higher-yielding US assets, pressuring the rupee further. The result is visible in the numbers above: FII outflows, a weakening rupee, and rising Indian bond yields, all moving together rather than offsetting each other.

What to Watch Today

The Fed decision, tonight. Rate call plus the dot plot at 11:30pm IST will set the tone for global risk appetite into Thursday's session here.

Whether Nifty holds the 23,100–23,300 zone. That's where options open interest is heaviest on the put side; a break lower would signal the support isn't holding.

Bond yields. A fifth straight weekly rise would confirm the RBI's bond sale and Fed jitters are compounding, not just coinciding.
