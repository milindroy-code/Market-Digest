Explainer

If the risks are the same as yesterday, why did the market rally today? Because a widely-expected event and a surprise event move markets very differently. Tonight's Fed hike, if it happens, has been "priced in" for days — reflected in bond yields, the rupee, and options prices well before the actual announcement. Markets often calm down right before a fully-anticipated event, precisely because there's little new information left to react to. What actually moves prices tonight won't be the hike itself, but the tone: the dot plot's signal on how many more hikes are coming, and whether the Fed sounds more worried about inflation or about growth. That's the real event to watch — not the headline rate change.

Strategy Corner
Concept of the day
Long Straddle

Buy a call and a put at the same strike and expiry. You profit if the underlying makes a big move in either direction — you're betting on volatility itself, not a direction. The cost is the combined premium of both options, which is also your maximum loss if the stock sits still. Traders reach for this ahead of binary events like a Fed decision or an earnings call — exactly the kind of setup tonight's Fed announcement represents.
