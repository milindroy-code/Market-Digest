Explainer

Is hafte poore teen din, FIIs bech rahe the aur DIIs khareed rahe the — same market mein, same din. Aisa kyun hota hai? FIIs (foreign institutional investors) — global pension funds, sovereign wealth funds, mutual funds — apna India allocation global risk appetite ke against decide karte hain: jab US yields badhte hain (jaise is hafte Fed hike ke baad), ya rupee weak hota hai, ek foreign investor ke liye same Indian stock ka dollar-return kam attractive ho jaata hai, chahe stock khud kuch na kare. Isliye woh sell karte hain — global reasons se, local news se nahi. DIIs — mutual funds, insurance companies, pension funds jo domestic savers ka paisa invest karte hain — us calculation se largely immune hain: SIP flows har mahine aate hain chahe market up ho ya down, aur unhe global yields ke against apna return compare nahi karna. Yehi wajah hai ki jab foreign flows kisi shock (Fed hike, oil spike) par nikalte hain, domestic flows usually stay ya barhte hain — is hafte, ₹10,212 crore DII buying ne ₹8,219 crore FII selling ko poori tarah absorb kiya.

Calendar Spread

Ek near-term option becho aur ek longer-dated option kharido, same strike par. Near-term option faster decay karta hai (time decay expiry ke paas aate-aate accelerate hota hai), isliye agar stock roughly flat rehta hai, position us decay-rate difference se profit kar sakti hai — ek strategy jo time ke around banayi gayi hai, direction ke around nahi. Is hafte jaisa pattern — ek sharp early-week shock, uske baad poora hafta ek range mein consolidate karna (Nifty last week ke close ke bahut paas hi wapas aa gaya) — exactly wo setup hai jahan traders time-decay-based strategies dekhte hain, ek clean directional bet ke jagah.
