After last week's strong +2.61%/+2.85% up-week, this week arrived in a completely different shape — a big Monday crash, a holiday, a sharp mid-week recovery, and then a Friday crude-driven reversal, leaving Nifty to close at 25,607.20 (↓193.40 points, 0.75% week-on-week), Sensex at 83,339.26 (↓580.45 points, 0.69%), against last Friday's (20 November) confirmed close of 25,800.60/83,919.71 — and all of that happened over just four trading sessions, since Tuesday (24 Nov) was a market holiday for Guru Nanak Jayanti. The week opened with a big shock Monday (23 Nov): Tata Steel's board approved acquiring a 26.4% stake in APL Apollo Tubes for ₹15,510 crore (~$1.67bn), alongside a 26% open offer and a reverse ₹28,000cr sale of Tata Steel's own tubes business — the news completely reversed a firm morning rally (day's high Sensex 84,662.96/Nifty 25,992.60), closing at Sensex 82,435.99 (-1,483.72/-1.77%), Nifty 25,349.65 (-450.95/-1.75%) — a ~2,227-point swing from the day's high to the close. Tuesday (24 Nov) was a full market holiday. Wednesday's (25 Nov) reopening brought a sharp catch-up rally — the morning's GIFT Nifty +0.96% gap-up signal extended through the day, closing at Nifty +469.60/+1.85% to 25,819.25, Sensex +1,502.29/+1.82% to 83,938.28 (both measured against Monday's close, since Tuesday was a holiday) — FMCG (+3.8%) and Financial Services (+2.17%) led, Metal (-2.04%) was the sole laggard; Jupiter Wagons surged ~20% on a South Korea metro-rail JV, while EMS stocks (Kaynes, Syrma SGS, Dixon) fell 3%+ on 2027 smartphone-shipment-target concerns. Thursday (26 Nov) saw gains continue but at a much more modest pace — Sensex +0.46%/84,328.59, Nifty +0.25%/25,884.50 (the week's highest close), with late selling trimming early gains; Nifty IT led (+3.66%: HCL Tech, BSE Ltd, TCS, Infosys, Tech Mahindra), FMCG lagged (-0.94%), and India VIX fell sharply to 16.82 (-4.81%). Crystal Crop Protection debuted at a modest ~2-3% premium (17x subscribed), and both FII and DII were net buyers for a second straight session. Then Friday (27 Nov) saw the week's gains begin unwinding: a mild morning gap-up signal had already faded by the Midday Pulse, and by the close Nifty was -277.30/-1.07% to 25,607.20, Sensex -989.33/-1.17% to 83,339.26 — snapping a two-session winning streak. The trigger: Brent crude crossing $97/barrel (+4.2% for the week) on reports of tanker attacks in the Strait of Hormuz and attacks on Saudi energy infrastructure, plus profit-booking after the week's rally. Bank Nifty fell 0.78% to 63,040.90 (confirmed), India VIX eased to 16.52 by week's end. Nifty IT was the week's weakest sector on Friday (-1.88%, giving back Thursday's surge), while Metal (+1.38%) — the week's biggest laggard on Monday — became Friday's strongest sector, a complete role reversal. Mahanadi Coalfields' ₹8,981-crore IPO (India's biggest coal-PSU listing) made a muted discount debut on Friday (NSE -1.47%, BSE -2.10%), with overall subscription of 1.52x.

Explainer

This week brought two big shocks — Monday's Tata Steel-APL Apollo deal crash and Friday's Hormuz-driven crude spike — but one fully reversed within two days, the other held. Why? Monday's shock was a single-stock, deal-specific event: the market reacted to the deal's complex structure and a lack of minority-shareholder clarity, but it was an isolated, one-time information event — once the market priced in the new facts (helped by a ready cushion of FII buying), the impact on index levels faded, and Nifty had already hit its week's highest close by Thursday. Friday's shock was different: crude oil was pricing in an ongoing, unresolved geopolitical risk (Hormuz tanker attacks), whose transmission runs through the whole economy — a weaker rupee, a bigger import bill, an inflation risk — not something confined to a single company. The distinction matters: a deal-specific crash can often turn into a buying opportunity once the uncertainty clears, but a geopolitical/macro shock tends to linger until the underlying risk itself resolves — in this case, until crude actually comes down from its $97 level.

Strategy Corner
This Week's Concept
Long Straddle

Buy a call and a put at the same strike and expiry. You profit if the underlying makes a big move in either direction — you're betting on volatility itself, not a direction. The cost is the combined premium of both options, which is also your maximum loss if the stock sits still. A week like this one — a Monday crash, then a sharp mid-week recovery, then a Friday reversal — is exactly the kind of setup where traders reach for a "big move is coming" position rather than a directional call. This week's pick from sequential rotation, carrying the high-volatility tag. This is purely an educational concept, not a recommendation to place the trade.
