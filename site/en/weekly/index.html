This week, Nifty finally did what it had only been "testing" for weeks — decisively clearing its long-watched 22,747/22,947 resistance zone, and holding above it. The start wasn't promising: Monday's positive gap-up open (on Trump's declared Russia-Ukraine "energy ceasefire" and softer crude) fully reversed through the day — against a backdrop of Houthi-Saudi tension, FII outflows and rising yields — and Nifty closed down 157.10 points (-0.70%) at 22,363.35. Tuesday brought two triggers at once — HCLTech's strong Q2 FY27 beat (profit +21% YoY, ₹12/share dividend) and Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman's Bloomberg New Economy Forum remarks that the government would "keenly" hear views on LTCG tax — pushing Sensex up 1,109.10 points (+1.54%) and Nifty up 358.60 points (+1.60%) to 22,721.95, just ~25 points short of resistance. Wednesday's hot September WPI print (11.39%, fuel-power +25.85% YoY) triggered profit-booking, and Nifty slipped 99.25 points (-0.44%). But Thursday-Friday rewrote the story: on Thursday, Indian markets directly reversed the overnight Wall Street/Asia selloff sparked by a hot US CPI print, with crude easing ~2% and IT/Private Bank strength driving Sensex +1,533.72 (+2.11%) and Nifty +374.65 (+1.66%) — decisively clearing the resistance zone; that same day, the Supreme Court upheld the quashing of Yes Bank's ₹8,415 crore AT1 bond write-off, crashing the stock -9% to -13%+. On Friday, a ~4% zoom in Nifty IT (led by Tech Mahindra's +7.75%) carried the breakout through its first full-session test — Sensex +413.70 (+0.56%)/Nifty +148.80 (+0.65%) to close. The week's net: Nifty at 23,146.15 (↑625.70 points, 2.78% week-on-week), Sensex at 74,729.27 (↑2,256.94 points, 3.11%) — a clean resolution, at least for now, to this digest's longest-running resistance storyline.

Global Cues

Crude and US inflation data were the week's two biggest cross-currents. Wednesday night's (US time) hot September CPI print (headline +3.7% YoY vs 3.6% expected, core +2.6%) triggered a tech-led selloff on Wall Street, which spilled over even more sharply into Asia on Thursday morning (KOSPI crashing -5.6% to -6%+, Nikkei sharply lower too) — AI-linked names (Nvidia, Oracle, CoreWeave) were hit hardest. But Indian markets reversed that entire fear on the same day (Thursday), aided by Brent crude easing ~2%. By Friday, Wall Street had also largely reversed its own selloff (Dow +1.08% to 52,177.69, S&P +0.33%, Nasdaq +0.08%) and Asia bounced (Nikkei +1.8%, KOSPI +2.72%) — though chip-stock stress wasn't fully resolved (the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index stayed lower, Europe's ASML -5.7%, ASM International -8.6%, Infineon -8.0%).

The rupee was a quiet positive story this week — following the RBI's 11 October rupee-stabilization steps (a dollar window plus hedging rules for three PSU oil-marketing companies), it closed up for a fifth straight session, reaching ~₹95.05/USD by Thursday, 183 paise stronger over the week — despite continued FII selling, as softer crude and a weaker global dollar outweighed that pressure.

Explainer

This week's biggest single-stock shock — Yes Bank losing at the Supreme Court — involved an instrument most retail investors rarely think about: AT1 (Additional Tier-1) bonds. These are special perpetual bonds banks issue to boost their capital buffer, and they carry a built-in "loss-absorbing" feature — if a bank's capital falls below a critical level, the regulator can write these bonds off entirely, without first wiping out shareholders. That's exactly what happened during Yes Bank's 2020 rescue — the administrator zeroed out ₹8,415 crore of AT1 bonds, over bondholders' objections. The Bombay High Court overturned that decision in 2023, but the Supreme Court has now quashed that very 2023 verdict — meaning the original write-off stands as final. It's a costly lesson for bondholders: AT1 bonds are called "bonds," but their risk profile sits much closer to equity — they promise a higher yield, but are also among the first instruments wiped out in a crisis.

Strategy Corner
This Week's Concept
Delta Hedging

Delta measures how much an option's price moves for a ₹1 move in the underlying stock. A clean technical breakout like this week's (decisively clearing the 22,747/22,947 zone) is exactly the moment large options desks most actively adjust their hedges — as the underlying moves, they have to buy or sell it to stay delta-neutral. It's an institutional-scale technique, not something retail can practically replicate directly, but it's worth knowing: when volumes suddenly spike on a breakout day, a good chunk of that can be hedging flow, not just directional buying or selling.
