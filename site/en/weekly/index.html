Explainer

This week brought four separate overnight shocks from Wall Street — a chip selloff, hawkish Fed minutes, then an IPO crash — yet Nifty and Sensex still closed the week up 2.6% and 2.85%. How does that decoupling happen? The first reason is composition: Nifty's biggest weights sit in financials, consumption and auto, not semiconductors or AI capex — so Nvidia-linked selloffs (Monday and Thursday nights) that hit US indices directly tend to have only a sentiment-level, one-day effect on Indian markets, not a structural one. The second is continued FII buying — ~₹14,926cr of net inflow across just three confirmed sessions gave the market a ready buying cushion after each gap-down. The third is timing: even Nvidia's own strong Q3 beat (Tuesday night) didn't show up in Indian markets the same day, because it landed after the US close — meaning Indian markets digest US news over a full trading day, not overnight. So when the US market looks like it's in "correction," that doesn't automatically mean Indian markets follow at the same scale — composition, liquidity, and timing all create separate decoupling effects.

Strategy Corner
This Week's Concept
Long Strangle

Like a straddle, but you buy a call and a put at different (out-of-the-money) strikes instead of the same one. It's cheaper to put on than a straddle because both legs are further from the money, but the underlying needs to move even further before you profit. A lower-cost way to bet on a big move without picking a direction. This week's pick from sequential rotation, high-volatility tag — and in a week where Nifty and Sensex saw two separate 1,300+/1,500+ point intraday round trips (Thursday and Friday both), a strategy that bets purely on "a big move happens" rather than on direction maps closely onto this week's actual price action. This is purely an educational concept, not a recommendation for any specific trade.
