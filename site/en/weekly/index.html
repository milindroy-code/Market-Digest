Explainer

The same mechanism showed up twice this week, in opposite directions — market-cap weighting. On Monday, Reliance Industries alone rose 11.5-11.6%, and because of its sheer size (₹17 lakh crore market cap) contributed roughly 198 Nifty points by itself — about half the week's biggest single-day move. On Friday, the opposite pattern appeared: the index closed 2%+ higher, yet 2,141 NSE stocks fell against just 1,439 that rose — still, three heavyweights (ICICI Bank, HDFC Bank, Bharti Airtel), because of their outsized index weight, contributed 240+ points between them and pulled the whole market higher, independent of what the other 2,000-plus smaller stocks were doing. Nifty and Sensex aren't "equal-weighted" — each stock's effect on the index is proportional to its market capitalisation, not an equal share. That's why reading the index number alone ("Nifty up 2%") can mislead — getting the real picture needs a look at breadth (advances vs. declines) and the broader indices (Midcap/Smallcap) too, as this week repeatedly showed.

This Week's Concept
Max Pain Theory

The idea that an underlying tends to gravitate toward the strike price at which option buyers, collectively, lose the most money by expiry — because option sellers (often larger, better-capitalized players) benefit from that outcome. It's a widely watched but imperfect signal, useful as one input among several rather than a guaranteed prediction. With a "concept" tag, this is this week's sequential-rotation pick — and after a week with this large a Q2 results haul and this many fresh Nifty highs, understanding options-market positioning (where theories like max pain apply) is especially relevant with the index trading this close to analyst-flagged resistance zones (24,700-25,500). This is an educational concept only, not a recommendation for any specific trade.
