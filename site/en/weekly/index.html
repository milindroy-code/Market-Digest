India VIX rose 4.15% over the week — a clear signal of rising trader nervousness. Friday alone saw the Sensex slip 120.83 points and the Nifty fall 79.70 points to close just under 23,400.

Global Cues

The story of the week is oil, not equities. Saudi Arabia shut its critical 1,200 km East–West crude pipeline — the one route that lets it bypass the Strait of Hormuz entirely — after drone attacks launched from Iraq on 10 September, with further strikes reported near the Strait itself. The pipeline normally carries roughly 4% of global oil supply.

Brent crude jumped more than 3.5% to above $107–108/barrel; WTI approached $103. US diesel prices crossed $6/gallon, a record high. Gold held at $4,461/oz on safe-haven demand, while the rupee stood at ₹95.56/$ — a weak currency alongside costlier crude is a combination that typically pressures both equities and inflation expectations here.

Wall Street moved the other way on Friday: the Dow gained 509 points (+0.98%) to 52,573.29, the S&P 500 rose 0.86% to 7,656.98, and the Nasdaq climbed 0.96% to 26,333.04 on cooler US inflation data — a positive cue that now sits in tension with the oil-driven risk-off mood at home.

Macro & Policy
  • RBI held the repo rate at 5.25% at its August review, a fourth consecutive pause. The next MPC meeting runs 5–7 October 2026 and will be watched closely given rising oil-driven inflation risk.
  • SEBI — from 1 September, every new single-holder demat account and mutual fund folio must carry a nomination or a signed opt-out declaration, under a circular dated 29 May 2026.
  • RBI — the special FCNR(B) dollar-rupee swap window closed 31 August; new NRI deposits no longer get the concessional pricing that had lifted rates to roughly 6–7%.
  • RBI + SEBI jointly launched Demat 2.0 at the Global Fintech Fest — a pilot testing tokenised corporate bonds on distributed-ledger and CBDC rails for faster issuance and settlement.

What to Watch — Week of 15–19 September

Crude oil's trajectory. The Saudi pipeline shutdown and Strait of Hormuz tensions are the single biggest swing factor right now, via inflation, the rupee and the current account.

Technical levels. Nifty broke below the 23,500 support zone; expect a 23,000–23,800 range, support near 22,800, resistance at 24,000–24,200. Reclaiming 23,700–23,900 would help confirm a recovery.

FII flow trend. Whether foreign outflows persist or ease as the oil shock evolves, and whether DIIs can keep absorbing the selling.

The IPO calendar. Four new issues open this week alone — primary-market appetite is a useful read on broader sentiment.

Run-up to the October RBI policy. Commentary ahead of the 5–7 October MPC meeting will be watched for early signals given rising oil-driven inflation risk.

Global divergence. A resilient Wall Street against a jittery, oil-sensitive Indian market is a gap worth watching into the week.
