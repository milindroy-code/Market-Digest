Explainer

This week both Alphabet and Amazon reported strong headline growth — yet both stocks fell, because investors fixated on "capex" and "free cash flow." Capex is the money a company spends building data centers, chips and infrastructure — a real cash outflow that doesn't show up as an expense on the profit-and-loss statement right away. When Alphabet spent $51.4bn and Amazon spent $77.3bn on capex in a single quarter — record levels for both — their free cash flow (operating cash minus capex) turned negative, meaning they're spending more cash than they're bringing in from the business, betting heavily on AI infrastructure. Investors' worry: if that AI investment doesn't convert into revenue quickly enough, margins get squeezed. That's why both stocks fell by double-digit percentages despite strong revenue growth — and that same theme spread across Asia, from Nikkei to the CSI 300.

Strategy Corner
This Week's Concept
The Iron Butterfly

A tighter cousin of the iron condor: sell an at-the-money call and put, and buy further out-of-the-money call and put as protection. It pays out most if the underlying finishes exactly at the middle strike, offering a higher potential reward than a condor for a market expected to stay very still — at the cost of a narrower profitable range. This week's pattern doesn't fully match its "range-bound" tag — the net move was a directional +2% — but the week's middle days (Tuesday's near-flat expiry, Wednesday's moderate red) would have suited a strategy like this better, while Thursday-Friday's big moves would have punished it had anyone actually been in the trade.
