A week that split into two very different halves. Monday through Wednesday brought a steady, multi-session recovery — crude cooled off, India's flash manufacturing PMI came in at 56.5, and Wall Street set fresh Nasdaq records. Then Thursday reversed all of it in a single session: Brent crude spiked off its multi-week low as US-Iran Strait of Hormuz strikes flared, the US 10-year Treasury yield hit its highest level since 2007, and a new IRDAI insurance-distribution reform proposal sent PB Fintech into a record ~30% single-day crash. Friday brought a modest, narrow stabilizing bounce, but Nifty and Sensex both ended the week net lower — the real story this week wasn't a clean trend, it was a sharp reminder that two unrelated shocks (geopolitical and regulatory) landing on the same day can wipe out a week's gains.

Global Cues

Wall Street's week also split into two halves. Monday and Tuesday night brought record-setting rallies — Nasdaq jumped 2.26% Monday to a fresh high of 27,122.09, then set another record close of 27,244.28 (+0.45%) Tuesday night. But the trend flipped Wednesday night: the 10-year Treasury yield's climb to 5.11% (highest since 2007) sent Wall Street lower for three straight sessions, with the yield reaching 5.225% and the 30-year yield touching 5.502% by Thursday-Friday. Friday night the Dow fell 0.7% (↓352.10) to 51,511.59, the S&P 500 fell 0.8% to 7,706.05, and the Nasdaq fell 1.1% to 26,936.04 — Utilities and Consumer Discretionary were hit hardest.

Brent crude's trajectory was a V-shape: it fell more than 2% Monday to ~$101/barrel (despite fresh Houthi-attack headlines, on West Asia de-escalation hopes), then eased further to its recent multi-week low of ~$99–101 by Tuesday-Wednesday, helping Wednesday's gains. Thursday's US-Iran Strait of Hormuz strikes spiked it from ~$102 to a $106.60 close Thursday night, and it held near ~$107 Friday as phased Hormuz-reopening talks continued in New York. The rupee stayed weak all week under crude/yield pressure, sliding from an early-week range of ₹95.5–95.9/$ to ₹96.1–96.2/$ by Friday. Gold held near its recent highs, at spot ~$4,338/oz Wednesday.

Explainer

Tata Sons' AGM put a spotlight on a company-law mechanism most people never think about: quorum. Any company's annual general meeting is only legally valid if a minimum number of members (or their authorized representatives) are present. If a major shareholder can't even nominate its own representative — as happened with the Sir Ratan Tata Trust (a ~23.56% stake), due to regulatory restrictions — the entire meeting can be deferred for lack of quorum, which is exactly what happened to Tata Sons on 18 August. Section 97 of the Companies Act, 2013 offers an escape route for precisely this situation: if a company can't convene its AGM with the required quorum, it can approach the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT), which can call the meeting itself or direct that it be convened, sometimes with reduced quorum requirements. This matters because the AGM is exactly where board reappointments — such as N Chandrasekaran's — get formally ratified; until the quorum issue is resolved, those appointments remain technically in limbo.

Strategy Corner
This Week's Concept
Position Sizing

Not every trade deserves the same amount of capital. Professional traders size a position based on how far their stop-loss is and how much of their total capital they're willing to risk on one idea — often just 1-2% per trade — rather than a fixed number of shares or lots. The strategy matters less than the size; a great setup with an oversized position can still wipe out an account. This week's pattern — a steady mid-week recovery reversed in a single session by Thursday's double shock (crude + IRDAI) — is exactly the kind of risk disciplined position sizing manages: a single bad day shouldn't be able to cripple an entire portfolio.
