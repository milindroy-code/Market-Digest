Explainer

When a company like Infosys gives its revenue guidance in "constant currency" (CC), it strips out the effect of exchange-rate moves — so even if the rupee and dollar swing 5% against each other, the CC growth number stays the same, because it measures only the real, underlying business growth, not currency noise. That's exactly why Friday's cut to Infosys' FY27 CC revenue guidance, from 1.5-3% to 1-2%, hit so hard — it wasn't a currency move, it was a genuine signal of softer demand. The number matters especially for IT companies, whose revenue comes in across multiple currencies — dollars, euros, pounds. Looking only at "reported" (currency-included) growth can make a weak quarter look fine thanks to favorable currency moves, or a strong quarter look bad because of adverse ones. CC guidance is what gives investors the real picture.

Strategy Corner
This Week's Concept
Delta Hedging

Delta measures how much an option's price moves for a ₹1 move in the underlying. A sustained, crude-driven volatility spike like this week's — India VIX climbing steadily, a single-stock shock like Infosys, and Nifty losing its own breakout zone — is exactly the environment where large options desks adjust their delta-hedges most actively: as the underlying moves, they have to keep buying or selling it to stay delta-neutral. This is an institutional-scale technique, not something retail can easily replicate directly, but it's worth understanding: in a volatile week, some of the extra volume you see can come from hedging flows, not just directional buying or selling.
