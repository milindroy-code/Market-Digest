"The market is a device to transfer wealth from those who cannot control their emotions to those who can."

— Attributed principle, markets wisdom

This week packed an entire mood swing into five sessions — and still barely moved the weekly scoreboard. Monday-Tuesday brought a genuine relief rally (the first green close after four red sessions, then a straight continuation), Wednesday saw the RBI raise its repo rate by 25 basis points to 5.50% — the first hike since February 2023 — and shift its stance to "calibrated tightening," which triggered a modest selloff. By Thursday, that pullback had turned into a full-blown crash: Brent crude spiked to above $104 on West Asia tensions, FII selling stretched to ten straight sessions (₹12,944 crore net sold on Thursday alone, this run's biggest single-day sell), and Nifty touched a fresh intraday low near 22,180. Then on Friday, TCS's strong Q2 FY27 results (revenue +11.2% YoY, profit +~15% YoY) drove Nifty IT up 3.02% and crude eased ~1% — the market clawed back much of Thursday's crash. The net result: Nifty closed at 22,520.45, just 98.50 points (0.44%) above last week's (1 October) close of 22,421.95, and Sensex at 72,472.33, up 562.63 points (0.78%) — a net-positive week that hides a 25-year-old-pattern rate hike, a 10-session FII selling streak, and day-to-day swings of 1%+ inside it. One necessary correction: the mid-week edition published during this run wrongly reported the RBI's decision as a "hold" and carried incorrect Nifty/Sensex closing levels for that day — this weekly's figures have been independently re-verified and carry the correct RBI hike and the correct closing levels.

Global Cues

Crude oil was this week's biggest swing factor. Brent jumped ~4% to above $104 on Thursday as West Asia tensions escalated, then eased 1% to $103.2/barrel on Friday (WTI -0.9% to $90.6) after US President Trump signalled no US strike on Iran before the 3 November midterms — reversing part of Thursday's spike.

Wall Street was also mixed this week — on Thursday night (8 Oct), the S&P 500 and Nasdaq fell for a second straight session (S&P -0.5% to 7,765.36, Nasdaq -1.3%) after a report that OpenAI's annualized revenue (~$50bn) came in well below the previously reported ~$70bn, rattling the AI trade (Nvidia -3%, Oracle -5.5%, CoreWeave ~-8%) — that weakness carried into a soft Asia session Friday morning, though GIFT Nifty signalled a modestly positive open, and TCS's results flipped the day's mood.

Explainer

The RBI didn't just raise the rate on Wednesday — it also shifted its "stance" to calibrated tightening, and that second move was the bigger signal for the market. The rate itself is just a number; the stance tells you what the central bank is likely to do next. "Accommodative" means cuts are more likely, "neutral" keeps both directions open, and "calibrated tightening" means the RBI has effectively taken rate cuts off the table for the near term, whatever its next move turns out to be. That's why a hike the poll consensus already expected (62% of economists called it correctly) still rattled rate-sensitive sectors like Realty, Auto and Banks — the market had already priced in the hike itself, but the confirmation that "cuts aren't coming any time soon" was a fresh, mildly hawkish surprise on top of it. It's the same forward-guidance logic the Fed and other central banks lean on — it's the signalled future path, more than the number, that moves prices.

Strategy Corner
This Week's Concept
Delta Hedging

Delta measures how much an option's price moves for a ₹1 move in the underlying. Large options traders and market makers buy or sell the underlying stock to offset their book's delta, staying roughly neutral to small price moves — it's how institutional desks can sell options in size without simply betting on direction. A week like this one (a hike-driven dip, an oil-driven crash, and an earnings-driven rebound, all inside five sessions) is exactly the environment where delta-neutral desks are busiest adjusting their hedges — this is an institutional technique that's hard to replicate directly at retail size, but understanding it explains how large players manage volatility like this week's.
