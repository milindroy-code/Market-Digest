The Sensex's gainers included Bharti Airtel, Trent, Maruti Suzuki, Power Grid and Eternal, while SBI, Axis Bank, TCS, Asian Paints and HDFC Bank were the biggest losers. Explainer: A Flat Headline, So How Did Smallcaps Gain 2.70%?

Today's most counter-intuitive fact: the Sensex fell just -0.01%, yet the Nifty Smallcap 100 gained +2.70% — a divergence of nearly 2.7 percentage points on the very same trading day. How is that possible? The Sensex and Nifty are both free-float market-cap-weighted indices, so their movement is driven by their 10-15 largest constituents — big banks, Reliance, IT majors. Today most of those heavyweights (SBI, Axis Bank, HDFC Bank, TCS, Asian Paints) were flat-to-lower, so the headline index barely budged. But the Midcap and Smallcap indices are far more diversified across hundreds of smaller companies, and when a focused rally — today's in Media, Realty and Pharma — happens outside those biggest names, it barely registers in the benchmark's math but shows up clearly in the Midcap/Smallcap indices. That's why a "boring," flat headline close can actually mask a good day for a diversified portfolio.

Today's Concept
Iron Condor

Sell an out-of-the-money call spread and an out-of-the-money put spread at the same time. You collect premium upfront and keep it if the underlying stays within a range through expiry — the trade that profits from a market going nowhere. Risk is capped on both sides, which is why it's a popular income strategy in sideways markets — a fitting concept for a narrow, near-flat session like today's, when the Sensex moved just -0.01%. This is purely an educational concept, not a recommendation to place the trade.
