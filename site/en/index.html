Explainer

Why should Indian markets care when Korean chip stocks fall? Samsung and SK Hynix aren't listed on the Nifty, but they're treated as among the cleanest proxies for the global AI trade — memory chips are a building block of AI data centers. When these stocks crash on a bubble scare, it reaches Indian markets through three channels. First, global risk sentiment: a "risk-off" mood can prompt FIIs to pull money out of emerging markets, India included. Second, Nasdaq's own AI-heavy composition — if Korea's selloff drags down US tech stocks too (as it did overnight), that flows straight into India through overnight US cues. Third, India's own IT companies (TCS, HCLTech, Infosys) are tied to the global tech-spending cycle — if doubts grow about AI capex, that sentiment can spill onto IT stocks even when their own fundamentals are strong (as HCLTech just showed). That's exactly the tension visible today, between GIFT Nifty's mild positive signal and Asia's sharp selloff.

Strategy Corner
Today's Concept
Long Straddle

A Long Straddle means buying a call and a put at the same strike and expiry. You profit if the underlying makes a big move in either direction — the bet isn't on direction, it's on volatility itself. The cost is the combined premium of both options, which is also your maximum loss if the stock sits still. Today's setup is exactly the kind of scenario where traders reach for this: GIFT Nifty is mildly positive on one hand, while a sharp AI-stock crash has swept through Asia and even reached the Nasdaq overnight on the other — direction isn't clear, but volatility is elevated. This is an educational concept, not a recommendation to place the trade.
