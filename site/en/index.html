"The big money is not in the buying and selling, but in the waiting."

— Jesse Livermore

The "rough Thursday" expected this morning — a hot overnight US CPI print (+3.7% YoY) driving a tech-led Wall Street selloff (S&P -1.1%, Nasdaq -1.9%) and an even sharper spillover into Asia (KOSPI crashing 5.6-6%+, Nikkei also sharply lower) — has fully reversed by midday in the Indian market. GIFT Nifty had signalled a flat-to-rough open around 22,600, but the market shrugged that off entirely: by 9:20 AM, the Sensex had gapped up 287 points to 73,068 and the Nifty 75 points to 22,696, led by buying in IT shares (Tech Mahindra, TCS, Infosys, HCLTech) despite the weak global cues. That gap-up built momentum through the morning — by 10 AM the Sensex was up 1.14% (73,610.88) with the Nifty IT index the top sector (+1.4%, TCS/Tech Mahindra/Infosys all up 1.5%+); and by a confirmed ~11:05 AM snapshot, the Sensex was up 1,011.82 points (+1.39%) at 73,793.71 and the Nifty up 294.60 points (+1.30%) at 22,917.30. That puts the Nifty already past and testing this digest's longest-running storyline — the 22,747/22,947 resistance zone — in the very direction that this morning's single, unconfirmed GIFT Nifty read had actually pointed toward. The rally has a name: the "reverse AI trade" — overnight selling in US AI-linked tech stocks has rotated money back into non-AI IT names, with Bharti Airtel also joining Tech Mahindra to lead the Sensex's 1,000+ point rally, per one headline. Easing crude oil prices, positive sentiment tied to Reliance's Jio IPO, and a strengthening rupee were additional tailwinds today. There's one clear outlier: Nestle India is down ~8% — the company's Q2 FY27 results are due today, but the exact timing and the specific reason for today's drop weren't independently confirmed in this run. No circuit filter or surprise policy move has been reported anywhere — today's real "breaking" story is this sharp, market-wide decoupling from overnight Asia's rout.

Strategy Corner
Today's Concept
Long Straddle

Buy a call and a put at the same strike and expiry. You profit if the underlying makes a big move in either direction — you're betting on volatility itself, not a direction. The cost is the combined premium of both options, which is also your maximum loss if the stock sits still. Traders reach for this ahead of binary events like a Fed decision or an earnings call — and today turned out to be exactly that kind of day: a "rough Thursday" expected at the open, and a 1%+ surprise rally by midday, precisely the size of unpredictable-direction move where betting on volatility beats guessing direction. This is an educational concept, not a recommendation to place the trade.
