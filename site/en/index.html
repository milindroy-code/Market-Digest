Today's session (Monday, 2 November) opens against Friday's (30 October) confirmed close, when the Sensex and Nifty posted the week's best day, before markets stayed shut for the weekend. On Friday the Sensex rose 308.49 points (+0.42%) to 74,459.53 and the Nifty gained 116.35 points (+0.51%) to 23,053.10, back above 23,000 — the day's intraday range was 74,082.03-74,742.59 for the Sensex and 22,905.10-23,118.10 for the Nifty. Bank Nifty diverged, falling 223 points to 54,687. For the week, the Nifty ended +2.00% and the Sensex +1.83% — almost exactly clawing back the prior week's -2.36%/-2.15% loss. This morning, GIFT Nifty is trading around 23,291, up roughly 205 points (+0.89%) from the previous Nifty futures close — a clear gap-up signal. The biggest reason: Reliance Industries announced its Q2 FY27 results after Friday's market close, and it was a big beat — consolidated net profit rose 40.6% YoY to ₹25,548 crore (from ₹18,165 crore), well above the ~₹20,900-21,368 crore analyst estimates cited in this digest's own Friday-morning edition. Revenue grew 27.8% YoY to ₹362,268 crore and EBITDA rose 24% YoY to ₹62,409 crore — the O2C segment led with EBITDA up 68% YoY to ₹25,203 crore, while the Retail segment's profit fell 24.3% YoY to ₹2,611 crore (revenue still up 5.8% to ₹80,220 crore, pointing to clear margin pressure there). A separate source cites a different profit figure (₹29,550 crore, +33.8%) that couldn't be reconciled this run, so the multiple-source-confirmed ₹25,548 crore/+40.6% figure is being treated as primary. Worth noting: RIL shares themselves were trading near a 52-week low (~₹1,160) ahead of the results — today's reaction is a key watch item. The same day, Jio Platforms (reporting its first quarterly result since listing on 28 October) posted standalone net profit up 14% YoY to ₹8,389 crore, with ARPU improving to ₹218.8 and EBITDA margin up 181bps — the stock had already closed Friday up 0.68% at ₹1,431.30 ahead of the results, about 28% above its ₹1,119 IPO price. Overnight, Wall Street saw a sharp selloff — the S&P 500 fell 1.82% to 7,580.07, 3.05% below its 6 October record high (7,818.93); the Dow fell 1.09% to 51,770.31 (-572.25 points); and the Nasdaq fell 2.86% to 25,437.00 — its worst single-day performance since June, capping an October that saw the Nasdaq drop 5.3%, its steepest monthly fall since March 2025. The selloff was driven by continuing AI-spending and cash-flow worries (a theme stemming from this week's capex-heavy Alphabet and Amazon results), compounded by month-end profit-taking and caution ahead of tomorrow's (Tuesday, 3 November) contentious US midterm elections. Asia saw a sharp tech-driven selloff on Friday (Nikkei -2.12%, Kospi readings ranging from -3.48% to -5.46%), but Monday morning shows partial stabilisation — the Kospi has recovered 0.92% to ~5,783, erasing its early losses, while the Nikkei is roughly flat (-0.02%, 63,278.66) and the Hang Seng is roughly flat (+0.03%, 23,301.74) — one pre-market reading had projected the Hang Seng about 0.2% lower, so sources show some inconsistency. Brent crude is at around $110.7/barrel today, down roughly 2.4-2.5% — a clear easing from the $114-118 range seen in recent editions. Gold is around $3,910/oz, though sources don't agree on direction (one reading shows +0.89%, another roughly flat) — flagging rather than guessing. USD/INR's fresh print wasn't confirmable this run; carrying forward the last confirmed level of ~₹95.21. On the broader corporate front, a wave of Q2 results landed today: NTPC (standalone profit +14% YoY to ₹5,320 crore), NTPC Green Energy (consolidated profit +56.2% YoY to ₹137.5 crore), CDSL (profit +24% YoY to ₹173 crore), Bharat Electronics (standalone PAT +19.8% YoY to ₹1,541 crore, above estimates), and Indian Oil Corporation, which reported its highest-ever quarterly standalone net profit of ₹21,630 crore — a massive swing from a ₹2,661 crore loss the previous quarter. On the other side, Sun Pharma's consolidated profit fell 36% YoY to ₹1,989 crore (from ₹3,118 crore) — a notable miss amid today's otherwise strong beats. SJVN's profit jumped from ₹308 crore to ₹657.4 crore, and Nazara Technologies announced a $77 million (~₹734.35 crore) investment in its wholly owned UK subsidiary. ITC, Marico, NALCO, NLC India, Container Corporation of India and Tata Investment Corporation are also scheduled to report Q2 FY27 results today.

Explainer: Why Is GIFT Nifty Signalling A Gap-Up After The Nasdaq's Worst Single-Day Drop Since June?

Overnight, the Nasdaq posted its worst single-day drop (-2.86%) since June, closing out an October that fell 5.3%. After a global selloff like that, a gap-down open for Indian markets would be the natural expectation. Yet today, GIFT Nifty is signalling a gap-up (+0.89%) — a reminder that Indian markets don't simply mirror global cues, but weigh them against their own domestic triggers. Two things explain this. First, the core driver of the US selloff is AI-capex and cash-flow concerns — a theme specific to US hyperscale tech giants (Alphabet, Amazon, Microsoft) that the Indian index has no direct exposure to. Second, and more importantly, Reliance Industries — the Nifty's single largest weight — delivered a big beat in its Q2 results on Friday (+40.6% YoY profit, well above estimates), a strong positive domestic catalyst that can outweigh an indirect, lagged global cue. This is an important lesson for Indian investors: global headlines set the market's broad mood, but domestic earnings and index-heavy stock news often override that direction — so the simple logic of "US markets fell, so ours will too" doesn't always hold.

Dollar-Cost Averaging (SIP)

Investing a fixed amount at regular intervals, regardless of price, rather than trying to time a single lump-sum entry. Known in India as a SIP, it removes the emotional guesswork of "is this the right time to buy" and has historically smoothed returns for long-term investors through volatile stretches — like the one playing out right now, with overnight's Wall Street selloff and GIFT Nifty's gap-up signal pulling in opposite directions.
