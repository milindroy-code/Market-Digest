Friday, 13 November's session began with a deepening selloff and stayed negative for most of the day, with only a modest recovery into the close trimming the day's worst losses. The market opened below yesterday's (12 Nov) confirmed close — Nifty 25,299.35, Sensex 81,911.01 — right from the 9:15 AM IST open: the Nifty down 0.35% at 25,209.85, the Sensex down 0.09% at 81,833.78. The selloff deepened quickly after the open — the Nifty touched an early low of ~25,077.55 (-0.87%) and the Sensex fell to ~81,300 (down more than 600 points), as flagged in today's own Midday Pulse. The decline deepened further around midday — one reading around 12:20 PM IST had the Nifty at 25,093.40 (-0.81%) and the Sensex at 81,389.16 (-0.64%) — before the market recovered some ground through the afternoon. Final numbers at the close: Nifty 25,144.30, down 155.05 points/-0.61%, Sensex 81,592.45, down 318.56 points/-0.39%, with the Bank Nifty also down roughly 484 points/-0.79% — levels cross-confirmed between Zee Business and Investing.com. India VIX kept falling too — between ~15.5-15.83 across two sources (down from yesterday's ~16.33-16.62) — a consistent signal that this selloff read less like panic and more like an overbought unwind layered with profit-booking.

The driver was clear: October CPI at 6.09% — above the RBI's tolerance band, a two-year high — triggered the heaviest profit-booking in banking, auto and IT heavyweights, as Zee Business reported, especially after the past few sessions' rally. Crude eased a bit through the day — Brent $95.36/barrel (-0.48%), WTI $88.01 (-0.82%) — down slightly from this morning's Trump-Iran-driven spike (~$95.45-95.70), though still elevated. By sector, Financial Services, Realty, Auto, Bank and Metal were laggards all day (Trendlyne's sector dashboard had Diversified as the weakest, -1.4%), while Power and Telecom saw buying into the close (Zee Business) — this morning's/midday's lone green sector, Energy, couldn't hold its gain through to the close.

At the stock level, today's biggest theme was earnings-reaction: Grasim Industries (-2.40% to -3%, ~₹3,010-3,040) fell after its Q2 FY27 results — the headline standalone PAT was up 187% YoY to ₹2,306 crore (on a record standalone revenue of ₹12,747 crore, +33%), but consolidated PAT actually fell 34.7% to ₹1,029 crore due to a one-off ₹825 crore impairment charge on its Canada pulp JV — the market priced in that consolidated weakness rather than the headline (today's Explainer covers exactly this). Tata Motors Passenger Vehicles also fell 3.08% to 3.37% despite strong revenue growth (consolidated ~+34-51% YoY, sources vary), as profitability landed at breakeven PBT on margin pressure. Other top losers included Shriram Finance (-3.47%), M&M (-2.13%) and UltraTech Cement (-1.65%). On the gainers' side: Eicher Motors (+1.34%) — whose board also approved its Q2 results today (exact PAT/revenue weren't confirmed this run, but the positive stock reaction suggests a decent print) — Bharti Airtel (+1.12% on the Nifty/+0.69% on the Sensex), HUL (+1.22%), Asian Paints (+0.87%) and Nestle India (+0.66%). In the midcap/largecap space, Hitachi Energy India extended yesterday's post-beat rally, up +10.7% to ₹31,595, alongside GE Vernova T&D India and PB Fintech (both +5.2%). ABB India, whose results were also due today per multiple results calendars, had no actual Q2 FY27 figures found in any search this run — it remains unconfirmed. Market breadth was negative — on the NSE, 1,797 stocks declined against just 1,253 advances (61 unchanged) — confirming an overall weak-breadth session despite the afternoon's partial recovery.

On currency and commodities, the rupee kept weakening through the day — USD/INR at ~₹94.30 (+0.27%), a third straight session of weakness from yesterday's ₹94.04 close. Gold (MCX) was at ₹1,36,765/10g (+0.4%), international futures at $3,903.17/oz (+0.46%); silver (MCX) was down a slight ~0.3%.

Explainer: Why A Profit Jump Of 187% Still Sent The Stock Down — Grasim's Case

Grasim Industries reported standalone net profit up 187% YoY to ₹2,306 crore today — a headline that should delight any investor. Yet the stock fell 2.4-3%. The reason: "standalone" is only the parent company's number; the consolidated figure — which includes subsidiaries and joint ventures — tells the fuller story. Consolidated PAT actually fell 34.7% to ₹1,029 crore, because Grasim took a one-off ₹825 crore impairment charge on its Canada pulp JV (AV Group NB Inc.) — essentially writing down the value of a past investment on its books, with no fresh cash loss, but a figure heavy enough to badly distort the reported profit number. Markets generally prefer to strip out such one-off items and look at "adjusted" or "core" earnings instead — Grasim's adjusted PAT was ₹3,132 crore, which is actually decent. But when the headline and the consolidated-reported number diverge this much, the market usually prices in the more conservative figure — which is why, despite a "+187%" headline, the stock itself reacted negatively.

Dollar-Cost Averaging (SIP)

Investing a fixed amount at regular intervals, regardless of price, rather than trying to time a single lump-sum entry. Known in India as a SIP, it removes the emotional guesswork of "is this the right time to buy" and has historically smoothed returns for long-term investors through volatile stretches like today's — a session that deepened its losses from morning through midday before recovering only modestly into the close.
