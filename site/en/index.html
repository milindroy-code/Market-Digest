Explainer: What GIFT Nifty Is, And How It Signals The Open

GIFT Nifty (formerly SGX Nifty) is a Nifty 50 futures contract that trades on the NSE IX exchange in GIFT City, Gandhinagar, and runs nearly round-the-clock — well before Indian markets open. Because it overlaps with global trading hours, it absorbs overnight US moves, Asian cues and breaking news, giving a directional hint before NSE/BSE open at 9:15 AM. Traders compare it against the Nifty's previous close: trading above that close signals an expected gap-up, below signals a gap-down. But it's only an indicator, not a guarantee — the actual opening price is set by NSE's own pre-open auction session, and a major news event between 9:00-9:15 AM can make the actual open diverge sharply from GIFT Nifty's signal. That's why this digest treats GIFT Nifty as a directional cue, not a confirmed number.

Strategy Corner
Today's Concept
Long Straddle

Buy a call and a put at the same strike and expiry. You profit if the underlying makes a big move in either direction — you're betting on volatility itself, not a direction. The cost is the combined premium of both options, which is also your maximum loss if the stock sits still. Traders reach for this ahead of binary events like a Fed decision or an earnings call — exactly the kind of setup in today's edition: the Fed's rate decision lands tomorrow, and today is also October's monthly F&O expiry. This is an educational concept, not a recommendation to place the trade.
