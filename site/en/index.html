Explainer: Sector Rotation — How The Dow Can Rise While The Nasdaq Falls On The Same Day

Tuesday's Wall Street close can look confusing at first — the Dow up 0.85% (back above 50,000), but the Nasdaq down 1.48% on the very same day. This is a classic case of "sector rotation": within one overall market mood (here, not risk-off, just profit-booking), investors move money out of one sector and into another, and broad indices can move in opposite directions because each index has a different composition. The Nasdaq is heavily weighted toward AI/chip stocks (CrowdStrike, TSMC, Oracle), where traders were booking profits after Monday's big rally — that same money flowed into industrials and healthcare (GE Vernova, Eaton, UnitedHealth), which carry more weight in the Dow. This isn't market-wide panic, just repositioning: "the AI trade got a bit overbought, and other places now look like better value." For India, if this rotation continues, it could mean renewed pressure on Nifty IT (which is itself heavily linked to global tech), while domestic-facing sectors (banking, FMCG, industrials) stay largely insulated from that pressure — exactly the kind of divergence we just saw between the Nasdaq and the Dow.

Today's Concept
Position Sizing

Not every trade deserves the same amount of capital. Professional traders size a position based on how far their stop-loss is and how much of their total capital they're willing to risk on one idea — often just 1-2% per trade — rather than a fixed number of shares or lots. The strategy matters less than the size; a great setup with an oversized position can still wipe out an account. Today's pick comes from the sequential rotation, tagged "concept" — a safe default when today's own direction is itself uncertain (a gap-up signal against mixed global cues). This is purely an educational concept, not a recommendation for any specific trade.
