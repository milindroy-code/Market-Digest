IPO & Corporate-Action Watch

This week's single biggest event is Jio Platforms' ₹30,213 crore mega-IPO — India's largest-ever IPO, price band ₹1,065-1,119 per share. Anchor investor bidding took place Monday (19 October) — pre-bidding reports flagged more than $20 billion in demand (global names like Fidelity, Allianz, Capital Group, GIC, BlackRock, alongside domestic anchors like SBI MF, LIC, ICICI Pru MF, HDFC MF and Kotak MF were reportedly in the mix), but final anchor-book allocation numbers haven't been published yet. Public subscription opens Wednesday, 21 October and closes Friday, 23 October — the net issue is split 50% for QIBs, 15% for non-institutional investors and 35% for retail. Exact allotment and listing dates will be confirmed once this window closes.

Explainer: From Anchor Book To Public Subscription — What Changes

The next step for Jio Platforms' IPO is the public subscription window opening — a quite different process from anchor bidding. Anchor investors (large institutional players) lock in their allocation a day before the issue opens, at a fixed price, and are subject to a lock-in period (30-90 days) — they can't sell immediately. The public subscription window has three categories bidding separately: QIBs (Qualified Institutional Buyers) — mutual funds, insurers, FPIs — who get 50% of the net issue in Jio's case; NIIs (Non-Institutional Investors) — HNIs and corporates, with 15% reserved; and Retail individual investors, with 35% reserved, applying in minimum lot sizes. Each category's subscription multiple (how much was applied for versus how much was on offer) is tracked separately — that number is what actually signals how strong demand is, while the GMP (grey market premium) offers an unofficial, pre-listing read on the same demand. A strong anchor book, as Jio's appears to be, is already a good sign for a mega-IPO like this — but the real test will be the public subscription numbers coming in from Wednesday through Friday.

Strategy Corner
This Week's Concept
Long Straddle

A Long Straddle means buying a call and a put at the same strike and expiry. You profit if the underlying makes a big move in either direction — you're betting on volatility itself, not a direction. The cost is the combined premium of both options, which is also your maximum loss if the stock sits still. A setup like this week's — Jio's mega-IPO public subscription opening, Infosys' Q2 results, and the question of follow-through in HDFC/Axis/ICICI — is exactly the kind of binary-event window where traders discuss high-volatility strategies like a straddle, even without a clear directional view. This is an educational concept only, not a recommendation to place the trade.
