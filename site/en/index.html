Explainer: What Is A "Retest"?

Today's analyst commentary repeatedly used one term — the Nifty is now on course to "retest" its August high of around 24,800. When an index first reaches a level (like 24,800 in August) and then falls back, that level becomes "resistance" — a psychological and technical ceiling where sellers tend to show up. When the market later returns to that same level, it's called a "retest," and it's a crucial moment: if the index decisively breaks through that resistance and holds above it, the old resistance becomes new support (technical analysts call this "role reversal"), seen as a strong bullish confirmation signal. But if the index gets rejected there again, that resistance becomes even stronger. That's why analysts are watching today's reclaim of 24,500 so closely — the next few sessions will decide whether this genuine six-session rally has the momentum to reach 24,800, or whether profit-booking stops it first.

Averaging Down vs. Pyramiding

Averaging down means buying more of a losing position to lower your average cost — risky, since you're adding to a trade that's already going against you. Pyramiding is the opposite: adding to a winning position as it moves in your favor. Most experienced traders favor pyramiding into strength over averaging into weakness, since it means the market is confirming the thesis rather than you fighting it. With a "concept" tag, this is today's pick — and a day like today's sixth straight green session, with breadth as strong as it was (2,547 advances vs 1,084 declines), is a textbook example of when the logic of pyramiding has the most appeal: when the market itself is confirming that a trend is genuine. This is an educational concept only, not a recommendation for any specific trade.
