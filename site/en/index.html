Explainer: Why RBI's New Domestic Money Transfer Rules Cap Cash Payouts And Mandate KYC

Domestic money transfer (DMT) services — the kind migrant workers use to send cash remittances back home — have long had a loophole: a remitter could make multiple small-ticket cash transactions without full KYC, something money-mule networks and layering (a money-laundering stage where illicit funds are broken into many small, legitimate-looking transactions to make tracing harder) could easily exploit. RBI's new Directions close that gap: a cap of ₹10,000 per transaction and ₹25,000 per beneficiary per month on cash payouts, along with mandatory KYC and additional-factor authentication on every transaction. This has almost no impact on genuine small remitters — say, a daily-wage worker sending salary home — since their typical transaction size sits well below those caps, but it makes it significantly harder for bad actors trying to route large volumes through cash. It's a routine compliance tightening rather than a crisis response, which is why the market has largely shrugged it off, as it did again today.

Strategy Corner
Today's Concept
Averaging Down vs. Pyramiding

Averaging down means buying more of a losing position to lower your average cost — risky, since you're adding to a trade that's already going against you. Pyramiding is the opposite: adding to a winning position as it moves in your favor. Most experienced traders favor pyramiding into strength over averaging into weakness, since it means the market is confirming the thesis rather than you fighting it. Today's pick comes from the sequential rotation, tagged "concept" — and a choppy, round-trip session like today's gives it a genuinely relevant frame: anyone who chased the morning's session high (82,013/25,316) would have felt the afternoon's giveback a lot more; trying to "rescue" that position by averaging down usually adds more risk, while pyramiding only ever depends on a confirmed move, not hope. This is purely an educational concept, not a recommendation for any specific trade.
