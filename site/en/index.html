Explainer: Why Is GIFT Nifty Flat When The Dow Fell 739 Points

Last night the Dow fell 739 points (-1.41%), and the Nasdaq fell 2.49% into correction territory — yet GIFT Nifty this morning is dead flat at -0.04%. That disconnect is real, and it has two causes. First, this was a narrow, chip-sector-concentrated selloff — names like Nvidia, Micron and Intel fell 4-8%, and it's precisely those AI/semiconductor stocks that carry the heaviest weight in the Nasdaq and S&P. Nifty's index composition is very different — financials and consumption sectors carry the most weight, and Indian IT's revenue comes from services/outsourcing contracts rather than chip capex, so it isn't directly exposed to this particular selloff. Second, November's record FII inflow — a net buy of ~₹89,856 crore in just the first 16 trading days — is building a domestic liquidity cushion that can absorb any imported risk-off mood. That's why last night's Wall Street crash arrives this morning as only a "flat, cautious" GIFT Nifty signal, not a matching crash.

Strategy Corner
Today's Concept
Protective Put

Buy a put option on a stock you already own, as insurance. If the stock falls, the put gains value and offsets the loss; if it doesn't, you've paid a small premium for peace of mind — the same logic as paying for car insurance you hope never to use. It's a common way to stay invested through an uncertain event without fully exiting a position — exactly the kind of setup today shows: both Nifty and Bank Nifty's RSI are stretched above 90, today is itself Nifty's weekly expiry, and the global mood has already turned risk-off ahead of Nvidia's own earnings (due after the US close today), following last night's 739-point Dow selloff. This is purely an educational concept, not a recommendation for any specific trade.
