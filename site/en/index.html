Explainer: Why A Cooling Global AI Trade Was Good News For Nifty IT

Asia saw a deep tech-selloff today — chip and AI-infrastructure-heavy names (like SoftBank, SK Hynix and Samsung, which have been at the core of this "AI trade" for weeks) fell sharply. Yet Nifty IT closed up 1.30% the same day — a direct contrast. The reason is simple: Indian IT majors (TCS, Infosys, HCL Technologies, Wipro) aren't part of that speculative AI-chip/AI-infrastructure trade at all — they're services/outsourcing companies that earn revenue from enterprise clients buying IT services, not from chip or AI-hardware capex. When the global AI trade cools off, two things happen: first, capital rotates out of that overheated, speculative trade into relatively cheaper, non-AI-bubble sectors — IT services is exactly the kind of sector that can benefit from this rotation. Second, if enterprises slow their AI-capex spending even slightly, they may redirect existing IT budgets back toward core services and legacy-system modernisation — which directly supports IT-services firms' revenue. That's why the same "global tech rout" headline can sink Asia's chip-heavy markets while Indian IT-services stocks close green the very same day — they're two different trades that just happen to share the word "tech."

Today's Concept
Long Straddle

Buy a call and a put at the same strike and expiry. You profit if the underlying makes a big move in either direction — you're betting on volatility itself, not a direction. The cost is the combined premium of both options, which is also your maximum loss if the stock sits still. Traders reach for this ahead of binary events like a Fed decision or an earnings call. Today's pick comes from the sequential rotation, tagged "high-volatility" — and today's session is a reasonably good fit for that tag: a sharp morning gap-down (day's low ~82,403, -0.74%) swung all the way to a 1%+ rally by the close (83,919.71), a full ~1,517-point intraday range. A long straddle profits from exactly that kind of big, two-way move, regardless of the final direction. This is purely an educational concept, not a recommendation for any specific trade.
