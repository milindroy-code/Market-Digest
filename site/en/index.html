Explainer: "AI Trade" Jitters — How Chip-Stock Doubts Spread Around The World

Over the past several weeks, a new phrase called the "AI trade" has been circulating through markets — it refers to the theme where big tech hyperscalers (Microsoft, Meta, Amazon and others) are pouring hundreds of billions of dollars into data centers and AI chips, much of it debt-funded. Whenever investors start doubting whether this massive spending will actually generate matching revenue or profit, a sell-off gets triggered — first in the companies directly tied to that spending cycle: chipmakers (SK Hynix, Samsung, TSMC-linked names), memory-makers, and data-center hardware suppliers, mostly listed in Korea, Taiwan and Japan. That's why days like today happen, with the Nikkei, Kospi and Hang Seng turning weak without any India-specific news behind it. India's direct linkage is small — no major chipmaker is listed here — but the effect still travels through two channels: first, a global risk-off mood can prompt FIIs to pull money out of emerging markets, India included; second, a pre-market indicator like GIFT Nifty directly mirrors global sentiment, which is why Asia's weakness is showing up today in GIFT Nifty's flat-to-negative signal, without any Indian company-specific trigger behind it.

Today's Concept
Bear Put Spread

The mirror image of a bull call spread: buy a put at a higher strike, sell a put at a lower strike. It reduces the cost of a bearish bet versus buying a put outright, at the cost of capping how much you can make if the stock falls sharply. After two straight red sessions, with crude elevated and GIFT Nifty signaling flat-to-negative, this concept is a reasonable fit for today's cautious mood. This is purely an educational concept, not a recommendation to place the trade.
