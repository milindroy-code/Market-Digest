Explainer: How Can One Stock Move The Whole Index This Much?

Nifty 50 is a free-float-adjusted market-cap-weighted index — each stock's weight is proportional to its total market value (counting only publicly tradable shares), not an equal share for every constituent. Reliance Industries, with its ₹17 lakh crore market cap, is one of Nifty's most heavily weighted constituents. When the stock rose 11.5-11.6% today, its rupee-value move was large enough that it alone contributed roughly 198 points to Nifty — about half of today's entire ~388-point move. That's why a single company's earnings beat, however large, can make the whole market feel like it's "rallying" even when the underlying breadth isn't quite as broad — which is why it's worth checking sector and breadth data alongside the index-level headline (as today's broader-market strength itself shows).

Strategy Corner
Today's Concept
Long Strangle

Like a straddle, but you buy a call and a put at different (out-of-the-money) strikes instead of the same one. It's cheaper to put on than a straddle because both legs are further from the money, but the underlying needs to move even further before you profit. A lower-cost way to bet on a big move without picking a direction. This is today's pick from the sequential rotation, with a "high-volatility" tag — a fitting setup for tomorrow's binary US midterm elections event, if someone were considering a hedge of this kind.
