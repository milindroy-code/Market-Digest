Today's session was one of the day's biggest reversal stories — a sharp morning gap-down turned into a decisive green finish by the close. Explainer: Why Can A Stock Rise Even When Profit Falls?

Today's most counter-intuitive move: Dr. Reddy's Laboratories' profit fell 36% YoY, yet the stock rose ~6.62%. How is that possible? Markets don't price a stock on the absolute number — they price it against expectations. Dr. Reddy's previous quarter (Q1 FY27) had seen a 69% YoY profit crash due to a one-time semaglutide-related provision, and management had guided for an H2 FY27 recovery at the time. This quarter's 36% decline — bad as it sounds — validates that trajectory: the decline is slowing, exactly as guided. When an actual result comes in "less bad than feared," or forward commentary turns positive, a stock can rise even when the headline YoY number is negative. It's a common pattern during earnings season — always check the number against the prior trend and guidance, not just the YoY headline.

Strategy Corner
Today's Concept
Max Pain Theory

The idea that an underlying tends to gravitate toward the strike price at which option buyers, collectively, lose the most money by expiry — because option sellers (often larger, better-capitalized players) benefit from that outcome. It's a widely watched but imperfect signal, useful as one input among several rather than a guaranteed prediction. With October's F&O expiry falling tomorrow (27 Oct), this concept is especially timely — price action around the max pain level is often more pronounced on expiry day. This is purely an educational concept, not a recommendation to place the trade.
