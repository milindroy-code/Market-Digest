Explainer: Why IT Was The Only Sector To Sink On A Hawkish-Fed Day

This morning's hawkish US FOMC minutes rattled the whole market, yet by the close only Nifty IT stayed in the red while the rest of the market closed up 1%+. The reason is simple: Indian IT majors (TCS, Infosys, HCL Technologies, Tech Mahindra) earn most of their revenue from the US and Europe — their business is directly tied to the US rate cycle, US corporate tech-spending budgets, and the dollar-rupee rate. When the Fed signals "higher for longer," US companies tighten their tech-spending budgets — that's a direct earnings risk for IT stocks, not just a sentiment effect. Auto and Bank, by contrast, are domestic-demand-driven sectors with a far more indirect link to US rates — what matters more for them is India's own consumption, credit growth and FII inflows, all of which stayed strong today. That's why the same "hawkish Fed" headline can produce two completely different reactions in one session — IT sinking while Auto and Banks surge.

Today's Concept
The Iron Butterfly

A tighter cousin of the iron condor: sell an at-the-money call and put, and buy further out-of-the-money call and put as protection. It pays out most if the underlying finishes exactly at the middle strike, offering a higher potential reward than a condor for a market expected to stay very still — at the cost of a narrower profitable range. Today's pick comes from the sequential rotation, tagged "range-bound" — and today's session is a direct mismatch with that tag: both Nifty and Sensex closed in a 1%+ trending rally, not a range-bound one. Flagging this transparently, since the rotation follows its sequence rather than cherry-picking by the day's actual conditions. This is purely an educational concept, not a recommendation for any specific trade.
