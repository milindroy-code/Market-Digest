Explainer: What India VIX Actually Measures, And Why Today's +6% Jump Matters

India VIX is calculated from the premiums of out-of-the-money Nifty options — it tells you how much percentage move the options market expects in the Nifty over the next 30 days, in either direction. It isn't a directional prediction, just a measure of expected magnitude. A quick rule of thumb: divide the VIX level by roughly 3.46 (the square root of 12 months) to get a rough expected monthly move — so a VIX of 14 implies the market is pricing in roughly a ~4% monthly swing. Today's VIX jumped ~6% alongside crude's climb to $114 and Infosys' mixed guidance cut — both add fresh uncertainty. A rising VIX isn't a guarantee of a crash, but it does show options buyers are paying more for protection, which makes it a useful sentiment gauge for traders.

Averaging Down vs. Pyramiding

Averaging down means buying more of a losing position to lower your average cost — risky, since you're adding to a trade that's already going against you. Pyramiding is the opposite: adding to a winning position as it moves in your favor. Most experienced traders favor pyramiding into strength over averaging into weakness, since it means the market is confirming the thesis rather than you fighting it. On a day like today — the fourth straight weak session, with the Nifty slipping further toward its support — is exactly the kind of setup where this distinction matters.
