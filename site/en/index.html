Explainer: What Is Anchor Investor Bidding?

When a mega-IPO like Jio Platforms holds "anchor investor bidding" two days before its public subscription opens, what's actually happening? Anchor investors are large institutional players — mutual funds, insurance companies, sovereign wealth funds — who, under SEBI rules, can be allotted shares a day before the IPO opens, at a fixed price that later forms part of the issue price. The benefit cuts both ways: the company locks in a large chunk of demand upfront, which builds credibility for the issue, and retail investors get an early signal of how smart money views it — when big names like leading mutual funds or sovereign funds take part in the anchor round, it's usually read as a positive sentiment indicator. But it's no guarantee — strong anchor interest doesn't always translate into strong listing-day performance, as several recent IPOs have shown. For a ₹30,213 crore mega-issue like Jio Platforms, today's anchor book is the first real-world signal of institutional appetite.

Today's Concept
The Iron Butterfly

A tighter cousin of the iron condor: sell an at-the-money call and put, and buy further out-of-the-money call and put as protection. It pays out most if the underlying finishes exactly at the middle strike, offering a higher potential reward than a condor for a market expected to stay very still — at the cost of a narrower profitable range. A day like today — where GIFT Nifty is sending a mixed/muted signal and the market is digesting weekend bank earnings without a clear directional bias — is exactly the kind of range-bound setup where this strategy comes up in conversation. This is purely an educational concept, not a recommendation to place the trade.
