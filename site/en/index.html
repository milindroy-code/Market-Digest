Macro & Policy

The RBI's new Foreign Exchange Risk Reserve (FERR) framework requires banks to hold cash reserves equal to 20% of the rupee value of eligible forex derivative contracts above $2 million — these reserves earn no interest, and the RBI's swap window covers only the FCNR(B) principal, so banks face rising deposit-interest hedging costs, compounded by sharply rising dollar-rupee forward premiums. Explainer: Why Did The Nikkei Fall On A Rate Hold — And Why Didn't Nifty IT?

A rate decision moves markets less through what it decides than through what it signals about the next one. Today the Bank of Japan held its policy rate at 1.25% — exactly as expected — but one board member voted for an immediate hike to 1.5%, the central bank raised its inflation forecast, and it signalled it's ready to tighten further, with economists now pricing a hike as soon as December or January. That "hawkish hold" framing, not the unchanged rate itself, is why the yen firmed. Separately, Japan's Nikkei still fell nearly 3% — not because of the BOJ, but because a fresh wave of selling in AI-linked chip and hardware stocks (the same worry that hit Alphabet and Amazon's results this week) spread through Asian tech. India's own IT majors, Wipro and Infosys among them, shrugged this off and led today's rally instead — because Indian IT services firms don't build chips or run AI data centres, so a global AI-infrastructure scare, oddly, can make them look like the safer, cheaper trade.

Today's Concept
Delta Hedging

Delta measures how much an option's price moves for a ₹1 move in the underlying. Large options traders and market makers buy or sell the underlying stock to offset their book's delta, staying roughly neutral to small price moves — it's how institutional desks can sell options in size without simply betting on direction. This is today's pick from the sequential rotation, with a "high-volatility" tag — the BOJ's dissenting vote and global tech volatility both fit that tag.
