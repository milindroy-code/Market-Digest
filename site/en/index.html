The market opened higher this morning on positive GIFT Nifty cues (+45.5 points to 22,648) and softer Brent crude (down ~1.5-1.9% to ~$103/barrel after Trump declared an "energy ceasefire" between Russia and Ukraine) — the Sensex up roughly 300-370 points, the Nifty up roughly 75-113 points. But by midday, that entire gain has been wiped out: as of ~12:30 PM IST (per Business Standard's live blog), the Sensex was down 568.06 points (-0.78%) at 71,904.27, and the Nifty down 151.15 points (-0.67%) at 22,369.30. This isn't just giving back the morning's gain — both indices are now below Friday's (9 Oct) confirmed close (Sensex 72,472.33, Nifty 22,520.45) as well. IT, FMCG, and banking shares are behind the reversal; the Nifty PSU Bank index alone is down ~1%. No circuit filter, surprise policy move, or fresh negative shock has been reported anywhere — but the reversal itself is today's real story, exactly the kind of thing the Midday Pulse exists to catch. One interesting counter-note: the RBI opened a special dollar window today for three PSU oil-marketing companies (taking their bulk dollar demand out of the spot market) and tightened some hedging rules, to support the rupee, which has been trading near its record-low zone (~₹96.96) and is down more than 7% this year — and the measures showed an effect in early trade, with the rupee strengthening to around ₹95.77/dollar even as equities slid. Two more big triggers are still pending: September's CPI data (not confirmed as released in this run) and HCLTech's Q2 FY27 results plus its third interim dividend decision, both expected after market hours.

Today's Concept
Dollar-Cost Averaging (SIP)

Dollar-cost averaging — known in India as a SIP — means investing a fixed amount at regular intervals, regardless of price, rather than trying to time a single lump-sum entry. It removes the emotional guesswork of "is this the right time to buy," and has historically smoothed returns for long-term investors through volatile stretches like today — a session that opened green and turned red by midday. This is an educational concept, not a recommendation to place the trade.
