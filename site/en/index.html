Explainer: Why An IPO Popping 38% Can Still Drag The Whole Market Down

When a highly-anticipated IPO opens above its issue price, it looks bullish at first glance. Anthropic's ANT stock went from an $83 IPO price to an intraday high of $115 — that's +38%. But what actually matters is the full session after the open, and here the stock faded its pop to close at just ~$97 (+17%). This is called "selling the news": when an event is so heavily anticipated that all the positive expectation is already priced in, the event itself becomes the trigger for profit-booking — early allocation holders lock in gains, and capital tied up in the new IPO also drains liquidity from other AI-linked names. This wasn't just one stock's story — investors treated it as a litmus test for the health of the whole "AI trade": if the year's biggest, most-anticipated AI IPO couldn't hold its own intraday momentum, that reinforces sector-wide overvaluation concerns. That's why one stock's intraday fade dragged the Dow/S&P/Nasdaq down more than 2%, and even pushed Nvidia to a fresh 52-week low.

Today's Concept
Dollar-Cost Averaging (SIP)

Investing a fixed amount at regular intervals, regardless of price, rather than trying to time a single lump-sum entry. Known in India as a SIP, it removes the emotional guesswork of "is this the right time to buy" and has historically smoothed returns for long-term investors through volatile stretches — like the one markets are in right now, after overnight's Wall Street-crash-driven risk-off. Today's pick comes from the sequential rotation, tagged "concept" — a safe default that happens to fit a volatile, uncertain opening like today's well.
