Movers & Corporate News

The market digesting Saturday's (17 October) Q2 FY27 results was today's dominant theme. Axis Bank fell the hardest — down as much as ~5% intraday — after its standalone profit of ₹7,003 crore (+38% YoY) came in below analyst estimates (~₹7,124 crore, per LSEG data); its NIM came in at 3.36%, with weak treasury income and higher costs also weighing. HDFC Bank fell ~2-2.2%, despite standalone profit rising nearly 5% YoY to ₹19,509.96 crore and NII growing 9.72%. Explainer: Why A Stock Can Fall Even After Profit Grows

Today HDFC Bank, Axis Bank and ICICI Bank all reported year-on-year profit growth — and all three stocks fell. The reason: markets don't just look at the headline profit number, they look at the quality of that profit and the forward guidance. The most important metric is Net Interest Margin (NIM) — the spread a bank earns on loans minus what it pays on deposits. When NIM is being squeezed (as at Axis Bank, at 3.36%, and at ICICI Bank, down 12 bps sequentially to 4.18%), the market treats it as a warning sign for future earnings, however good this quarter's profit number looks. Similarly, when profit growth is cushioned by one-off items or weak treasury income (as flagged at Axis Bank), analysts discount the "core" earnings accordingly. That's why experienced investors don't just react to a "profit up X%" headline — they track the NIM trend, management guidance and the quality of earnings behind the number.

Today's Concept
Max Pain Theory

Max Pain Theory holds that an underlying (like the Nifty) tends to gravitate toward the strike price at which option buyers, collectively, lose the most money by expiry — because option sellers (often larger, better-capitalized players) benefit from that outcome. It's a widely watched but imperfect signal: useful as one input among several, not a guaranteed prediction. The stale max-pain reading flagged in today's Technicals section (13 October's baseline, 22,550) is exactly the kind of figure this concept describes.
