Explainer: What Does "Correction Territory" Actually Mean?

Today's Nasdaq 100 is a good example: when an index falls 10% or more from its recent peak, market-speak calls it a "correction" — smaller than a "bear market" (a drop of 20% or more) and typically shorter-lived. The threshold isn't a magic number, but a widely used convention for signalling a normal process of unwinding overvaluation. Importantly, a correction is neither a cause of recession nor a reliable predictor of one — US markets have seen 30-plus corrections since 1980, most of which resolved without any major economic slowdown following. In today's case, the Nasdaq 100's correction is AI/semiconductor-specific (capex and valuation concerns), even as the broader US economy (per the JOLTS data) still looks resilient. So an index "entering correction territory" isn't a reason to panic by itself — what matters more is understanding exactly why the fall is happening.

Today's Concept
Bull Call Spread

Buy a call at a lower strike and sell a call at a higher strike, same expiry. This caps both your cost and your maximum profit compared to buying a call outright — you give up unlimited upside in exchange for a lower breakeven. This is today's pick from the sequential rotation, tagged "uptrend" — but today's fit is less clean than yesterday's: after a four-session rally, GIFT Nifty's signal has now turned flat-to-negative, and the US midterm elections' final outcome also remains pending. If the underlying uptrend (as seen over the last four sessions) holds and today is just a short pause, a Bull Call Spread remains a way to express a moderately bullish view cheaply — but given today's subdued opening signal, it would be wise to wait for trend confirmation before any entry.
