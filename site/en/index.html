Explainer: What Is A "Circuit Breaker," And Why Did Korea's Trigger One Today?

South Korea's Kospi fell so fast this morning that its exchange triggered a circuit breaker — a 20-minute full trading halt. A circuit breaker is an automatic safety mechanism: when an index falls more than a pre-defined threshold (say, -8% or -10%) in a single day, the exchange temporarily halts trading, giving panic-selling a chance to pause and investors time to absorb new information, rather than letting order flow run on pure momentum. India has a similar system — the NSE/BSE trigger market-wide halts of varying duration when Nifty or Sensex move 10%, 15% or 20% — but India's market structure typically isn't as concentrated or leveraged as chip-heavy Korea's, where large institutional and retail-margin positions sit concentrated in a handful of stocks (Samsung, SK Hynix, and the like). That's part of why a sharp, sector-specific global shock like today's tripped a circuit breaker in Korea while India hasn't triggered one — a reminder that the same global news event can hit different markets with very different severity, depending on their own structure.

Strategy Corner
Today's Concept
Delta Hedging

Delta measures how much an option's price moves for a ₹1 move in the underlying stock or index. Large options traders and market makers buy or sell the underlying to offset their book's delta, staying roughly neutral to small price moves — it's how institutional desks can sell options in size without simply betting on direction. This is today's pick under the "high-volatility" tag, and the fit is direct: the VIX jumped ~70% overnight, and India VIX was also moving up yesterday — in conditions like this, option premiums rise sharply, and market-makers' delta-hedging activity can itself add extra volatility to the underlying market (as they buy or sell against large stock-moves to keep their own position neutral). That's useful for retail investors to understand too — a sharp, global-event-driven gap-down day like today can artificially inflate option premiums, making directional option-buying expensive and risky. This is purely an educational concept, not a recommendation to place the trade.
