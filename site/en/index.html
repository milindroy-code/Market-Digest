Explainer: Markets Are Shut — So Why Does Today's US CPI Still Matter?

When NSE/BSE close for a holiday, it doesn't mean the Indian market "pauses" — global events keep happening regardless, and their impact simply gets delayed to the next trading session. That's exactly what's happening today: the US October CPI report lands this evening (IST), and Wall Street will react to it immediately — if inflation comes in hotter or cooler than expected, US stocks, bond yields and the dollar will all move overnight. That move gets captured directly through GIFT Nifty (the Nifty futures contract traded on the Singapore Exchange, which stays open outside Indian market hours), so when NSE opens Wednesday morning, it will find that overnight sentiment already priced in. A "market holiday," in other words, means today's news flow is being stored up for tomorrow, not disappearing — which is why an investor shouldn't stop watching the news just because the exchange is closed for the day.

Today's Concept
Covered Call

If you already hold a stock, selling a call option against it is called a "Covered Call." You collect the premium as income, and if the stock stays below the strike, you keep both the stock and the premium. The tradeoff: if the stock rallies hard past the strike, you miss out on gains beyond it, since you're obligated to sell at that price. A safe, sequential pick tagged "any" — a fitting theme for a genuine market-holiday day like today, when there's no live price action, yet discussing how to generate steady income from an existing holding stays relevant. This is purely an educational concept, not a recommendation to place a specific trade or pick a strike.
