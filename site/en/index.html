Explainer: Why A US Inflation Number Can Shake Indian IT Stocks

On Friday, US October PPI (producer price inflation) came in at 6.5% YoY — the hottest print since Nov 2022 — and in response AMD fell 8.75% and Nvidia fell too. PPI is a leading indicator of consumer inflation, so a hot print raises the odds the Fed holds rates higher — or even hikes — in December, pushing up US bond yields (currently ~5.15%). AI/tech stocks are the most sensitive to this move, because their valuations are priced on earnings far out in the future — a higher discount rate shrinks the present value of those earnings the most, so these stocks fall disproportionately. That effect reaches India through two channels: first, global fund managers trim tech-sector risk everywhere, including Indian IT majors (TCS, Infosys, HCL Tech, Wipro) whose revenue comes from the same US corporates. Second, a stronger dollar/higher US yields pull FII money away from emerging markets like India, adding pressure on the rupee too. That's the chain that takes a Friday US inflation print all the way to today's "IT under pressure" sector-watch note.

Strategy Corner
Today's Concept
Long Straddle

Buy a call and a put at the same strike and expiry. You profit if the underlying makes a big move in either direction — you're betting on volatility itself, not a direction. The cost is the combined premium of both options, which is also your maximum loss if the stock sits still. Traders reach for this ahead of binary events like a Fed decision or an earnings call — exactly the kind of setup today's session shows: a hot US PPI print, Fed-rate uncertainty, and a global AI/tech-stock selloff together make a genuinely high-volatility backdrop, with this week's FOMC minutes and Nvidia's earnings still to come. This is purely an educational concept, not a recommendation for any specific trade.
