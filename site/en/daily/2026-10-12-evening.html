Explainer

What is a "sell-on-rise" session, and how is it different from a straight selloff? A straight selloff happens when the market opens with negative sentiment already baked in and stays lower all day. A "sell-on-rise" session is different: the market opens on positive cues (as today's GIFT Nifty and softer crude were), even climbs to a higher level, but on every rally larger players sell into it — they believe the upside is capped, or that underlying concerns (like today's Middle East tension and FII outflows) haven't been priced in yet. The result: an intraday high forms, then selling pressure gradually dominates through the rest of the session, until the index closes below even its own open — exactly what happened to the Nifty today, from a high of 22,651.10 to a low of 22,304.45. This pattern often signals that market confidence is fragile — every bounce is being treated as an exit opportunity rather than fresh buying conviction.

Today's Concept
Max Pain Theory

Max Pain Theory holds that an underlying (like the Nifty) tends to gravitate toward the strike price at which option buyers, collectively, lose the most money by expiry — because option sellers (often larger, better-capitalized players) benefit from that outcome. It's a widely-watched but imperfect signal, useful as one input among several rather than a guaranteed prediction. A sell-on-rise session like today's — where the Nifty faded from a higher open to close lower — is exactly the kind of scenario where traders use the max-pain level as a reference point, to gauge whether price action is genuine or also being shaped by options positioning. This is an educational concept, not a recommendation to place the trade.
