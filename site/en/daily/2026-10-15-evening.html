And the day's biggest single-stock shock came from elsewhere: the Supreme Court upheld the Bombay High Court's 2023 judgment quashing Yes Bank's ₹8,415 crore AT1 bond write-off, dismissing Yes Bank's own appeal — the stock crashed (reports range -9% to -13%+ by the close, with intraday swings going deeper still), with market cap falling to roughly ₹60,000 crore. IDBI Bank also fell 8% (to ₹83.07) on news of a Cabinet-approved stake sale.

Explainer

What are AT1 bonds, and why is Yes Bank's Supreme Court case such a big deal? AT1 (Additional Tier-1) bonds are a special kind of bank debt that help a bank meet RBI's capital-buffer rules. Their most distinctive — and riskiest — feature: if a bank runs into serious financial stress, the regulator can convert them into equity or write them off entirely (to zero value), sometimes even ahead of shareholders, flipping normal corporate-bond logic on its head. In 2020, during RBI's reconstruction of Yes Bank after its own financial crisis, ₹8,415 crore of AT1 bonds were written off completely — bondholders got nothing. They challenged this in court, arguing it violated RBI's own master circular and the bonds' information memoranda. The Bombay High Court ruled in their favour in 2023; today the Supreme Court upheld that ruling, dismissing Yes Bank's appeal. That means Yes Bank may now have to compensate these bondholders — the exact amount isn't clear yet, but the market immediately priced it as a major financial-liability risk, sending the stock down double digits.

Strategy Corner
Today's Concept
Long Strangle

Like a straddle, but you buy a call and a put at different (out-of-the-money) strikes instead of the same one. It's cheaper to put on than a straddle because both legs are further from the money, but the underlying needs to move even further before you profit. A lower-cost way to bet on a big move without picking a direction. Today's index-level move was clear (up), but single-stock volatility was high — Yes Bank fell double digits in a single day after a Supreme Court verdict, Nestle India fell despite a strong beat. It's exactly around such binary/surprise events (a court verdict, quarterly results) — where a big move is expected but the direction is hard to call — that a strategy like the long strangle becomes useful. This is purely an educational concept, not a recommendation to place the trade.
