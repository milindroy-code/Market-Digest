Explainer

Why does the US 10-year Treasury yield hitting 5.11% matter for Indian markets? The 10-year Treasury yield is the world's "risk-free" benchmark — the rate against which every other investment, from stocks to mortgages, gets valued. When it rises, US bonds — already considered among the safest assets — start offering an even better return without taking on extra risk. That pulls global investors, especially out of emerging markets, back into US Treasuries: the dollar strengthens, and outflows pick up from both EM stocks and bonds. This is directly relevant for India: FII selling pressure can build, the rupee can weaken, and the global cost of capital resets higher, which weighs on stock valuations too — especially for sectors whose business is tied to US clients or dollar-denominated debt, like IT. Analysts are now watching whether 5% turns out to be a "ceiling" or just a "waypoint" — some investors are already talking about 6%.

Strategy Corner
Today's Concept
The Iron Condor

Sell an out-of-the-money call spread and an out-of-the-money put spread at the same time. You collect premium upfront and keep it if the underlying stays within a range through expiry — the trade that profits from a market going nowhere. Risk is capped on both sides, which is why it's a popular income strategy in sideways markets. Today, India VIX is near its multi-month lows (10.34) and Nifty's options max pain (23,450) sits very close to spot — both point to a tight, range-bound setup. But a caveat: today's Trump-Xi summit is a genuine two-sided event risk that could break the range in either direction — so range-bound strategies need to be weighed carefully against the event's outcome.
