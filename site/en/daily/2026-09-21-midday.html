Falling crude oil is being cited as the biggest driver of today's gains, easing inflation and margin worries. But the move isn't broad-based: Tata Group stocks are the one place where selling is continuing, even as index-level gains are largely coming from FMCG, Pharma and Realty.

Today's Big Story: The Market's Up, Tata Stocks Are The Outlier

Today's most interesting pattern is a divergence: Nifty and Sensex are near the day's highs, helped by falling crude oil, with FMCG/Pharma/Realty leading — but Tata Group stocks are moving the opposite way at the same time. Tata Chemicals was the biggest loser in morning trade, down roughly 3%, while Tata Power, Tata Investment and Tata Technologies were down about 2% each, and TCS and Tata Motors, down around 1% at the open, were recovering toward flat. The underlying cause is the same as it's been — the Tata Sons-Tata Trusts standoff over N Chandrasekaran's reappointment — but today it has picked up a new angle: the Shapoorji Pallonji Group has floated a proposal to monetize part of its Tata Sons stake for at least ₹25,000 crore. That's a move by a third major stakeholder in Tata Sons' unlisted structure, and it widens the dispute beyond just Tata Trusts and management.

Strategy Corner
Today's Concept
Risk-Reward Ratio

Before entering a trade, compare what you stand to lose (distance to your stop-loss) against what you stand to gain (distance to your target). A trade with a 1:3 risk-reward ratio can still be profitable overall even if you're wrong more often than you're right — this is why professional traders talk about expectancy, not just win rate. On a divergent day like today — index up, Tata Group down — the risk-reward on either side of that split looks very different, and that's what should decide whether a trade makes sense at all.
