Explainer

A weak jobs report came out Friday — so why did Wall Street rally instead of falling? It sounds counter-intuitive, but the market's logic is straightforward: investors don't just look at how many jobs were created, they look at what that data means for the Fed's next move. When jobs data comes in weak (29,000 jobs versus an 84,000 estimate, unemployment rising to 4.2%), it signals a cooling labour market — which pushes the Fed away from a rate hike and toward holding or cutting. Because stocks, especially rate-sensitive tech names, directly benefit from lower rates (cheaper borrowing, a higher present value on future earnings), the market treats weak jobs data as "good news for rates," even though it's "bad news for the economy." That's exactly why the Nasdaq posted its biggest gain Friday — a small, counter-intuitive mechanism that plays out on almost every jobs-report day. The relevance for India is direct: if US rates are headed lower-for-longer, it narrows the widening yield gap that's been pulling FIIs out of Indian equities and into dollar assets.

Today's Concept
Risk-Reward Ratio

Before entering a trade, compare what you stand to lose (distance to your stop-loss) against what you stand to gain (distance to your target). A trade with a 1:3 risk-reward ratio can still be profitable overall even if you're wrong more often than you're right — this is why professional traders talk about expectancy, not just win rate. A day like today — the RBI MPC's Day 1 and a triple IPO listing happening together — is exactly the kind of setup where carefully defining your risk-reward before entering new positions becomes worthwhile, a concept that's educational, not a recommendation to place the trade.
