Technicals & Positioning

The biggest open question across several past editions — whether the Nifty would hold its broken 22,747/22,947 level as support — now has an answer, and it's "no." Thursday's session closed straight through the entire zone, at 22,231.80, after touching a fresh 52-week intraday low of 22,179.90. This is a classic "bull trap" pattern — when a breakout fails to hold and price falls straight back below the prior range. Options data is mixed: one source puts PCR at 0.63 with max pain at 22,400, another at 0.83 with max pain at 22,450 — both point to roughly neutral-to-bearish positioning. Near-term support now sits in the 22,000-22,200 zone, and 22,747 is the level that's now resistance again, which bulls would need to reclaim to regain control.

Explainer

How does a "resistance breakout" fail and turn into a "bull trap"? When price closes above an established resistance zone, traders read it as bullish — the hope is that what was once resistance now becomes support. But that's only confirmed when price "retests" that level and bounces off it. If the retest fails — meaning price falls straight back below the entire zone, as the Nifty did yesterday, going from 22,747/22,947 straight to 22,231.80 — it becomes a "bull trap": traders who went bullish late, chasing the breakout, are now sitting on losses, and their forced selling can push the price down further still. That's exactly why experienced traders never treat a single close as breakout confirmation — they wait for a successful retest, and when one fails, exiting quickly is usually the right call.

Today's Concept
Bear Put Spread

The mirror image of a bull call spread: buy a put at a higher strike and sell a put at a lower strike, same expiry. This reduces the cost of a bearish bet compared with buying a put outright, at the cost of capping how much you can make if the stock falls sharply. Yesterday's breakout reversal, the FII selling streak now at 10 sessions, and global AI-stock jitters together paint a clear downtrend setup. For traders with a bearish view on further downside who don't want to pay the full cost of a naked put, this is a fitting structure. This is an educational concept, not a recommendation to place the trade.
