The Fed did exactly what markets expected — and Wall Street sold off anyway. Overnight, the Fed raised its key rate 25 basis points to 3.75%–4%, the first hike since 2023, but Chair Kevin Warsh's hawkish press conference and a dot plot signalling at least one more hike this year sent the Dow down over 600 points. Asia mostly shrugged the news off overnight, but GIFT Nifty is pointing to a soft, gap-down open in India this morning — a reminder that even a "priced-in" event can still move markets hard once its actual details land.

Global Cues

The Fed hiked 25 basis points to 3.75%–4% on a unanimous vote — the first hike since 2023. But stocks fell during Chair Kevin Warsh's press conference, as his comments were read as more hawkish than expected — he said it was hard to call overall financial conditions "restrictive." The updated dot plot showed 16 of 18 officials expecting at least one more hike this year, with four expecting two more. The Dow fell 631.21 points (1.21%) to 51,461.90; the S&P 500 slid 0.45% to 7,551.81; the Nasdaq dipped 0.01% to 25,978.42 — essentially flat; the 10-year Treasury yield moved back above 5%.

Asian markets mostly absorbed the hawkish hike overnight without much drama: Japan's Nikkei rose 0.87% and the Topix 0.71%, South Korea's Kospi gained 1%, Australia's ASX 200 added 0.16% — while Hong Kong's Hang Seng fell 0.49% and China's CSI 300 slipped 0.16%. Brent crude eased from Tuesday's $109.2 peak to around $108.2/barrel. The rupee weakened on dollar strength to around ₹95.95/$ (from ~95.84), while gold held at an elevated $4,340–4,350/oz.

Explainer

If the hike was priced in, why did the market fall on the news? Because "priced in" only covers the headline number — markets also trade on the Fed's guidance about what comes next, and that's where the surprise was. Ahead of the meeting, traders broadly expected one more hike this year; the Fed's own dot plot showed 16 of 18 officials expecting at least that much, with four expecting two more. That's a more hawkish path than bond and equity markets had already priced in — which is why yields jumped and stocks fell, even though the 25bp move itself was no surprise at all.

Strategy Corner
Concept of the day
Long Strangle

Like a straddle, but you buy a call and a put at different (out-of-the-money) strikes instead of the same one. It's cheaper to put on than a straddle, but the underlying needs to move even further before you profit — the kind of move last night produced, when even a widely-anticipated event still delivered a sharp swing.
