Explainer

Why does Trump rejecting a geopolitical proposal end up moving India's GDP forecasts? The Strait of Hormuz carries a huge share of the world's seaborne oil and LNG trade — India imports roughly 88% of its crude, and about two-thirds of that crude plus half its LNG travels through routes crossing Hormuz. When talks around this chokepoint break down — as happened over the weekend when Trump rejected Iran's proposal — crude prices immediately price in a disruption-risk premium, even without any actual physical supply block. For India, the transmission is straightforward: costlier crude widens the trade and current-account deficit, weakens the rupee, and feeds imported inflation. Analysts now estimate a sustained $10/barrel crude rise could widen India's CAD by 40-50bps — which is exactly why Q2 FY27 GDP growth forecasts are being trimmed from ~7.7% toward ~6.7%. A single rejected proposal, transmitted through oil, ends up repricing the entire macro outlook.

Today's Concept
Delta Hedging

Delta measures how much an option's price moves for a ₹1 move in the underlying. Large options traders and market makers buy or sell the underlying stock to offset their book's delta, staying roughly neutral to small price moves — it's how institutional desks can sell options in size without simply betting on direction. On a day like today — with Bank Nifty's monthly F&O expiry in play and GIFT Nifty's signal running mildly positive against Monday's broad rout — it's exactly this market-maker delta-hedging flow around expiry that can produce sudden, sharp intraday swings without any fresh fundamental news.
