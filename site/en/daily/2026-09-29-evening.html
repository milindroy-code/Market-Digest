Technicals & Positioning

More significant than today's close (22,716.20) itself is that Nifty tested its 200-week moving average (~22,600) — the first time since the Covid crash of 2020. That's a major long-term support zone; a decisive break below it could open the door to the next support around 22,222. On the upside, resistance sits at 22,700-22,750 (the max pain zone, with heavy Call open interest), then 23,000-23,120 and 23,200-23,450 (key EMAs). Max pain shifted from 22,600 to 22,750 through the session, and the PCR improved from a weak 0.65 in the morning to around 0.77-0.91, suggesting traders added Puts at lower strikes — a sign of some hedging/short-covering interest despite the fall. Today was also the monthly F&O expiry for both Nifty and Bank Nifty (with LIC Housing Finance and SAIL under an F&O ban), which added to closing-hour volatility.

Explainer

Today's biggest technical story matters more than the headline number: Nifty tested its 200-week moving average (200-WMA) — the ~22,600 level — for the first time since the Covid crash of March 2020. The 200-WMA is a long-term trend indicator: an average of roughly the last four years of weekly closing prices, which filters out short-term noise to show a big-picture support/resistance zone. As long as price stays above this line, the long-term bull trend is technically considered intact; a first decisive test of this line is a warning sign that a lot of long-term holders' cost basis is close by, which can increase selling pressure if the level breaks. It's no guaranteed predictor — Nifty touched this same level in 2020 too, and then recovered sharply — but traders and analysts watch it closely because, historically, institutional buying interest tends to show up around levels like this.

Today's Concept
Dollar-Cost Averaging (SIP)

Investing a fixed amount at regular intervals, regardless of price, rather than trying to time a single lump-sum entry. Known in India as a SIP, it removes the emotional guesswork of "is this the right time to buy" and has historically smoothed returns for long-term investors through volatile stretches like the one markets are in right now. A session like today's — where Nifty is testing its 200-week moving average amid geopolitical uncertainty — is exactly the kind of moment where this discipline pays off most: following a consistent, rule-based approach instead of reacting to a single day's move.
