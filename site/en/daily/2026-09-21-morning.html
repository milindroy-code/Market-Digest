Explainer

Tata Trusts says Chandrasekaran's reappointment is "void ab initio" — what does that actually mean, and how can a board vote that already happened be undone weeks later? "Void ab initio" is a Latin legal term meaning "void from the beginning" — it doesn't mean the resolution was valid and later got cancelled; it means it was legally invalid from the very moment it was passed, as though it never happened at all. Tata Trusts' argument rests on procedural and quorum technicalities: it contends the reappointment was never validly passed in the first place, since it remains unresolved who represents Tata Trusts' 66% stake in Tata Sons for voting purposes. That's very different from a valid resolution that gets challenged in court and might later be overturned — if Tata Trusts' legal position holds, Chandrasekaran's second term simply never legally existed at all. That's why the dispute is heading toward a shareholder vote at Tata Trusts' own AGM rather than litigation, where its 66% stake could decide the matter directly — and why the volatility keeps landing on the *listed* group companies (Tata Chemicals, TCS) rather than on Tata Sons itself, which isn't listed at all.

Strategy Corner
Concept of the day
Position Sizing

Not every trade deserves the same amount of capital. Professional traders size a position based on how far their stop-loss is and how much of their total capital they're willing to risk on one idea — often just 1-2% per trade — rather than a fixed number of shares or lots. The strategy matters less than the size; a great setup with an oversized position can still wipe out an account. Whether the headline index opens with today's gap-up or Tata-linked volatility comes right back, a professional trader's reflex isn't just "what to trade" — it's "how much to size it."
