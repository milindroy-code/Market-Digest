Explainer

₹6-7 lakh crore was wiped out today — but not a single extra barrel of crude actually stopped flowing through Hormuz. So why did markets move so hard? Stock markets don't price actual events — they price the probability of future events. When Iran offered a seven-day proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz on Friday, markets had already partially priced in de-escalation — that's why crude cooled and Nifty bounced last week. When Trump rejected that proposal over the weekend and told aides strikes could resume after the midterms, markets had to reverse their entire "de-escalation" assumption — on a headline alone, with no actual supply disruption. That's why Brent jumped toward $108 today and FIIs kept selling: they now have to price elevated geopolitical risk over the coming weeks, not just today's fear. These "expectation reversal" moves are often sharper than actual physical disruptions, because the whole market reprices its assumptions at once, together.

Strategy Corner
Today's Concept
Averaging Down vs. Pyramiding

Averaging down means buying more of a losing position to lower your average cost — risky, since you're adding to a trade that's already going against you. Pyramiding is the opposite: adding to a winning position as it moves in your favor. Most experienced traders favor pyramiding into strength over averaging into weakness, since it means the market is confirming the thesis rather than you fighting it. On a sharp red day like today, when many investors are tempted to "average into" their favorite names, this distinction matters especially — adding to a falling position and adding to a confirmed trend are two very different decisions.
