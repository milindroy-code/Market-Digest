Explainer

Trump's tariff threat hasn't gone away — so why did pharma stocks jump today? Because price moves aren't driven just by whether a risk exists, but by how much of it is already "priced in." The pharma index had fallen for five straight sessions on the overhang of proposed US generic-drug tariffs, until stocks became genuinely oversold — creating an entry point for value buyers. The underlying risk hasn't disappeared: as analysts have pointed out, the 0% tariff on generic medicines holds until 31 July 2028, meaning this threat is still years away, not immediate. Today's bounce is a signal that sentiment is cooling off after an oversold correction — not that the actual risk has been resolved. That distinction matters: a stock rising because it got "cheap" and a stock rising because the "problem got solved" are two very different stories.

Strategy Corner
Concept of the day
Covered Call

If you already hold a stock, sell a call option against it. You collect the premium as income, and if the stock stays below the strike, you keep both the stock and the premium. The tradeoff: if the stock rallies hard past the strike, you miss out on gains beyond it, since you're obligated to sell at that price. For a flat, rotation-heavy day like today — where the headline index barely moved — this is a popular way to squeeze extra income out of positions you're already holding.
