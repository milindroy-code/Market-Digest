Explainer

What does the RBI's "neutral stance" mean, and why does it matter as much as the rate number itself? When the RBI announces its policy, it's not just the rate number that matters — the "stance" attached to it matters just as much. "Neutral" means the RBI's next move could go either way, depending on incoming data. "Accommodative" means the RBI is biased toward supporting growth (leaning toward rate cuts), while a hawkish tilt or "withdrawal of accommodation" means it's prioritizing inflation-fighting (leaning toward more hikes). That's why, when tomorrow's (Wednesday's) outcome lands, markets won't just ask "was there a 25bp hike or not" — they'll parse the stance's exact wording just as closely, because a "hike with a neutral stance" signals a one-off move, while a "hike with a hawkish tilt" signals more could follow. That's why experienced market-watchers read the full policy statement, not just the headline number.

Strategy Corner
Today's Concept
Delta Hedging

Delta measures how much an option's price moves for a ₹1 move in the underlying. Large options traders and market makers buy or sell the underlying stock to offset their book's delta, staying roughly neutral to small price moves — it's how institutional desks can sell options in size without simply betting on direction. A day like today — Day 2 of the RBI's MPC, with the outcome tomorrow — is exactly the kind of setup where hedging against volatility becomes a relevant concept, one that's educational, not a trade-placement recommendation.
