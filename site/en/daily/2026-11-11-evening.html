Wednesday, 11 November's session drew a complete arc — a morning gap-up, a bigger midday extension, and a steady afternoon fade — yet still closed solidly green. This morning's GIFT Nifty signal (~+1.1-1.2% gap-up against Monday's 25,044.65 close) fully materialised and then extended further: Business Standard's live blog's ~12:00 PM IST update had the Sensex 1,246.43 points (+1.54%) higher at 82,353.08, with the Nifty up 332.95 points (~25,377.60) — as this morning's Midday Pulse had already flagged. That high didn't hold — with IT and pharma stocks weighing, the market steadily gave back gains through the afternoon, and by the close the Sensex was roughly 470+ points off its day's best level. Final numbers: Sensex 81,879.40, up 772.75 points (+0.95%), Nifty 25,255.05, up 210.40 points (+0.84%) — against Monday's (9 November) confirmed close of 81,106.65/25,044.65 (Tuesday was a market holiday for Balipratipada) — levels cross-confirmed across HDFC SKY, Kotak Neo and ChiniMandi. Sector leadership also rotated through the day: while the morning's gap-up was banking-led (Bajaj Finance, Asian Paints, ICICI Bank were the early top gainers), by the close it was Nifty PSU Bank (+2.94%) out front, with Nifty Bank (+1.76%) and auto stocks also strong and FMCG posting solid gains — while Nifty IT (-0.53%), Nifty Realty (-0.42%) and Nifty Pharma (-0.41%) all closed mildly red, the very sectors that triggered the afternoon's pullback. Explainer: Why Falling Crude Oil Is Such A Big Deal For India

India imports roughly 85% of its crude oil needs — so every move in global oil prices flows straight through to the country's import bill, its current account deficit (CAD), the rupee and inflation. When Brent slides, as it did to Tuesday's two-month low (after Trump's Iran-conflict remarks), it means India spends fewer dollars to import the same volume of crude — improving the CAD and easing pressure on the rupee. One analyst's rule-of-thumb estimate (not an official government figure) puts it at roughly $1.5-1.6 billion of CAD improvement per $1/barrel fall in Brent — a useful number for scale, even if not exact. That's part of why Indian markets kept rallying today despite Wall Street's overnight slide on a hot US CPI print — cooling crude and continued FPI buying ($5.68bn this month, the best since September 2024) offset the global headwind. Whether crude holds this recovery or slides back down, either way it's directly relevant to Indian markets.

Position Sizing

Not every trade deserves the same amount of capital. Professional traders size a position based on how far their stop-loss is and how much of their total capital they're willing to risk on one idea — often just 1-2% per trade — rather than a fixed number of shares or lots. The strategy matters less than the size; a great setup with an oversized position can still wipe out an account. Today's pick comes from the sequential rotation, and it's a genuinely relevant reminder for a session like this one — anyone who chased the midday peak momentum with an oversized position would feel the afternoon's giveback a lot more without disciplined sizing. This is purely an educational concept, not a recommendation for any specific trade or strike selection.
