Explainer

How is the rupee strengthening even while FIIs keep selling? Foreign Institutional Investors have now sold Indian stocks for 15 straight sessions — that usually pressures the rupee, because the money FIIs pull out of stocks typically leaves the country in dollars, cutting demand for rupees. Yet the rupee closed Thursday on its fifth straight gaining session, near ₹95.05/USD. How? Three other forces are currently outweighing FII selling. First, crude oil prices are softening (Brent near $104-105/barrel) — India imports most of its oil, so cheaper crude directly eases the rupee's demand-supply balance, since importers need fewer dollars. Second, the dollar itself is weakening globally as traders unwind "long dollar" positions. Third, the RBI is intervening to support the currency. The takeaway: no currency moves on a single input — FII flows are just one factor, and crude prices and the global dollar trend matter just as much.

Today's Concept
Bull Call Spread

Buy a call option at a lower strike, and sell a call at a higher strike, same expiry. Compared with simply buying a call outright, this caps both your cost and your maximum profit — a cheaper way to express a moderately bullish view, trading unlimited upside for a lower breakeven. Today's setup — where the Nifty has decisively cleared its long-watched 22,747/22,947 resistance and GIFT Nifty is signalling a firm continuation, even as the global chip-stock picture stays mixed — is a classic Bull Call Spread scenario: a way to express a capped, near-term bullish target without taking on full directional risk. This is an educational concept only, not a recommendation to place the trade.
