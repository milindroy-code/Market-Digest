Markets started the new week on a firm note — Nifty and Sensex closed higher, helped chiefly by a sharp drop in crude oil, with FMCG, Pharma and Realty leading the sectoral gains. But once again, Tata Group stocks swam against the tide, extending a second straight session of declines after the Shapoorji Pallonji Group's proposal to monetise part of its Tata Sons stake for at least ₹25,000 crore kept the group's governance standoff in focus. Separately, NSE's own ₹22,562 crore IPO closed for subscription today, with demand surging through the final hours.

Global Cues

Wall Street closed out a volatile, Fed-hike-dominated week on a mixed note Friday night — the Dow slipped 95.40 points (0.18%) to 51,682.64, while the S&P 500 edged up 0.17% to 7,650.50 and the Nasdaq gained 0.39% to 26,522.55. The bigger domestic story today was crude: Brent fell 2.3% to around $101.5/barrel and WTI dropped 2.4% to $97.9, as markets weighed a fresh Houthi attack on Saudi Arabia against signs that the kingdom has been restoring disrupted export flows. The rupee firmed slightly to ~₹95.88/$ (from ~₹95.95), while gold pulled back from its recent highs — MCX gold slipped toward ₹1.54 lakh/10g and spot gold eased 0.44% to around $4,363/oz, as improved risk appetite reduced some safe-haven demand.

Explainer

Why does a falling crude oil price move Indian stock markets so much? India imports more than four-fifths of the crude oil it consumes, so the price of oil isn't just an energy story here — it's a macro story. A cheaper barrel shrinks India's oil-import bill, which helps narrow the trade deficit and takes pressure off the rupee. It also flows straight into inflation: fuel and transport costs feed into almost everything else in the CPI basket, so cheaper crude gives the RBI more room to stay accommodative. And at the company level, oil is a direct input cost for sectors like aviation, paints, tyres and FMCG packaging, so their margins improve almost immediately. That's why a 2% move in Brent crude, as happened today, can move the whole market's mood — even when nothing has changed in any individual company's business.

Strategy Corner
Today's Concept
Trailing Stop-Loss

Instead of a fixed stop-loss, a trailing stop moves up as the price rises, locking in gains while still giving the trade room to run. It's a way to let winners run — a piece of trading wisdom you'll see quoted often — without needing to predict exactly when a trend will end. On a day like today, with Nifty and Sensex extending their advance and closing right at a flagged resistance band, it's a reminder that riding a trend doesn't require calling its top — just protecting the gains already made while staying in the position.
