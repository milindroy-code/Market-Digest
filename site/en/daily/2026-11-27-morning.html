Explainer: Why Markets Can Rally Before The Bad News Is Even Ruled Out

Yesterday, Asia and Europe both closed strongly higher — a relief signal for Dalal Street too — simply because a proposed US tariff may now come in lower than the previously flagged 25%/17%, somewhere near 15-20%. Worth noting: that isn't a confirmed, final number, yet the market has already priced it in. That happens because markets default to pricing in the worst case until something else is confirmed — as long as a 25% tariff risk is alive, stocks discount that full downside. The moment a credible signal arrives that the actual number could be lower (even while still unconfirmed), the market immediately re-prices that reduced risk — even without an official announcement. This is why traders say "markets move on expectations, not outcomes." The risk here: if the final number comes in at 25% again (or India isn't in that "capped" group after all), today's relief rally could reverse just as quickly.

Today's Concept
The Iron Butterfly

A tighter cousin of the iron condor: sell an at-the-money call and put, and buy further out-of-the-money call and put as protection. It pays out most if the underlying finishes exactly at the middle strike, offering a higher potential reward than a condor for a market expected to stay very still — at the cost of a narrower profitable range. Today's pick from the sequential rotation, tagged "range-bound" — a reasonable fit for today's actual conditions: after three sessions of aggressive rallying, yesterday's session (+0.25-0.46%, with late selling trimming gains) and this morning's merely-mild GIFT Nifty signal both point to the market moving into a calmer, narrower-range phase — exactly the environment in which an Iron Butterfly is traditionally considered. This is purely an educational concept, not a recommendation to place the trade.
