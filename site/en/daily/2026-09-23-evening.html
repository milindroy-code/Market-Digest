Explainer

Why does a Trump-Xi summit move Indian markets at all? Rare earths — 17 chemical elements used in smartphones, EV motors and military radar — are abundant globally, but China controls around 70% of their mining and roughly 90% of processing. Last year, in response to US tariffs, China curbed rare-earth exports, sending shockwaves through global supply chains. That vulnerability is why the Trump administration has adopted a warmer tone heading into the summit — predictable access to Chinese rare earths reduces the urgency of building expensive alternative supply chains. This matters directly for India: if the summit produces a concrete tariff or rare-earth deal, it could slow or accelerate the pace of the "China+1" supply-chain shift — a major factor for India's manufacturing and export ambitions, not just a headline geopolitical event.

Strategy Corner
Today's Concept
The Long Strangle

Like a straddle, but you buy a call and a put at different (out-of-the-money) strikes instead of the same one. It's cheaper to put on than a straddle because both legs are further from the money, but the underlying needs to move even further before you profit. A lower-cost way to bet on a big move without picking a direction. Tomorrow's Trump-Xi summit — whose outcome could swing anywhere from tariffs to rare earths — is exactly the kind of genuine two-sided event where traders consider this strategy.
