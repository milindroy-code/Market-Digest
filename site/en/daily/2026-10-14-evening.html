Explainer

What's the difference between WPI and CPI, and why does the market react to a WPI jump when the RBI itself targets CPI? WPI (Wholesale Price Index) tracks prices at the wholesale level — what producers and wholesalers charge each other, before goods reach the consumer. CPI (Consumer Price Index) tracks the retail level — what the end-consumer actually pays in the shop. The RBI officially targets CPI, not WPI, for its repo-rate decisions. Yet the market still reacts to a WPI jump because it acts as a leading indicator — when wholesale prices rise today, that cost can pass through to retail prices (CPI) in the coming months, especially when an input like fuel or crude carries a heavy weight directly in WPI. Today's 11.39% print worried markets for exactly this reason — the fuel-and-power segment's 25.85% year-on-year jump could push retail inflation higher in the months ahead, pushing the RBI's rate-cut expectations further out.

Strategy Corner
Today's Concept
Dollar-Cost Averaging (SIP)

Investing a fixed amount at regular intervals, regardless of price, rather than trying to time a single lump-sum entry. Known in India as a SIP, it removes the emotional guesswork of "is this the right time to buy." A mixed-signal day like today — the broader index down on a WPI jump and FII selling, even as the IT sector keeps rallying — is exactly the kind of scenario where timing a single entry becomes hardest. SIP/DCA has historically smoothed returns for long-term investors through volatile stretches like this one. This is an educational concept, not a recommendation to place the trade or make the investment.
