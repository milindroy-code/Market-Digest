Explainer

India doesn't produce most of its own crude — so why does a Middle East chokepoint move Indian stocks? Roughly a fifth of the world's oil passes through the Strait of Hormuz every day, and India sources roughly 45-50% of its crude/LNG imports through that same route. When tension around the strait rises, Brent jumps almost immediately — as it did last night, crossing $106. For India, that means three hits at once: costlier crude directly hits refining margins and inflation, a bigger oil import bill weakens the rupee (currently around ~₹96/$), and companies with crude-linked input costs — paints, tyres, airlines, FMCG — see their margins squeezed. That's why today's pull-back in Brent from its $108 peak to ~$107 — a small-looking move — matters: it signals the panic has cooled a bit, not that it's fully over.

Strategy Corner
Today's Concept
Calendar Spread

Sell a near-term option and buy a longer-dated option at the same strike. The near-term option decays faster — time decay accelerates as expiry approaches — so if the stock stays roughly flat, the position can profit from that difference in decay rates. It's a strategy built around time itself, not direction. Today's bounce brought Nifty back into a narrow range — between 23,000 support and the 23,345-23,465 resistance zone — without confirming a clear trend either way. In a range-bound setup like this, where no big near-term directional move is expected, a time-based strategy like the Calendar Spread becomes more relevant than a directional bet.
