Friday, 20 November's session is measured against yesterday's (Thursday, 19 November) confirmed close — Nifty 25,514.30 (+286.35 pts/+1.14%), Sensex 83,021.75 (+989.94 pts/+1.21%), its best single-day gain of November. The gap-down flagged in today's Morning Briefing materialized — a direct spillover from the overnight Wall Street selloff (the Dow closed below 50,000 for the first time since June, Nasdaq -2.25%) and Asia's deep-red open (Nikkei -4% to -4.5%, Kospi -4.35% to -4.77%). Opening reports show slightly different levels (one early tick puts the Sensex at ~82,839 (-175 pts), another a few minutes later at 82,558.25 (-463.50 pts/-0.56%)) — suggesting the selling deepened fast right after the open. By ~9:17 AM IST the Sensex had fallen to a day's low of ~82,403 (-615 pts/-0.74%), with Nifty at ~25,345 (-170 pts/-0.66%). From there, a partial recovery set in — by ~11:30 AM IST the Sensex was at 82,707.44 (-314.31 pts/-0.38%), Nifty at 25,422.40 (-91.90 pts/-0.36%), meaning roughly half the day's loss has reversed. But market breadth doesn't fully back this recovery — 970 stocks advancing vs. 1,997 declining, suggesting the bounce is largecap/index-level rather than broad-based. There's no circuit filter or surprise policy move — the overnight global-selloff spillover, alongside a sharp parallel gold/silver crash, are today's two breaking items. Domestic 24K gold fell ~4% to Rs 1,22,435/10g, silver -6% to Rs 1,48,034/kg — both at their lowest levels of 2026. Anindya Banerjee (Kotak NEO) said the selling has now spread from commodities to equities and crypto. India VIX was also up 5% at 16.90 at the open — in line with the expectation flagged in yesterday's edition. One small divergence: Brent crude is slightly higher (~$92.2-92.7, +0.3% to +1%) despite the broader risk-off — tanker disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz are cited as a factor.

Strategy Corner
Today's Concept
Max Pain Theory

The idea that an underlying tends to gravitate toward the strike price at which option buyers, collectively, lose the most money by expiry — because option sellers (often larger, better-capitalized players) benefit from that outcome. It's a widely watched but imperfect signal, useful as one input among several rather than a guaranteed prediction. Today's pick comes from the sequential rotation, tagged "concept" — and a two-way, volatile session like today's, where an early-morning crash has already seen a partial reversal, is exactly the kind of scenario where traders watch max-pain levels as one extra data point heading into expiry. This is purely an educational concept, not a recommendation for any specific trade.
