Today's session split into two different stories — the headline indices closed green for a fourth straight day, helped by banking, infra and energy plus cooling crude oil, but the day's real drama was a Tata Group stock crash. Just yesterday, Tata Sons' board had voted 4-1 to give N Chandrasekaran another five-year term, and the stocks rallied; today Tata Trusts (which owns ~66% of Tata Sons) declared that resolution "void," and names like Tata Chemicals, TCS and Tata Motors Passenger Vehicles fell 3-8%. Separately, Hero Motors' final subscription day closed today, and NSE's own IPO closed Day 2.

Explainer

The same Tata Sons news sent stocks up yesterday and down today — why? Because what actually moves the price isn't "what happened," it's certainty about "who's in control." Tata Sons itself isn't listed, but listed companies like Tata Chemicals and Tata Investment Corporation hold direct stakes in it — a balance-sheet asset whose value is tied to Tata Sons' own valuation. When the board gave Chandrasekaran another term yesterday, the market treated it as resolved, and that embedded value got re-priced upward — stocks rose. When Tata Trusts (which itself owns ~66% of Tata Sons) declared that same resolution "void" today, the control question reopened — and the stocks with the most direct Tata Sons exposure, Tata Chemicals most of all, fell the hardest. It's a reminder that in holding-company structures, uncertainty itself carries a price, even when the underlying business hasn't changed at all.

Today's Concept
Bear Put Spread

The mirror image of a bull call spread: buy a put at a higher strike, sell a put at a lower strike. It reduces the cost of a bearish bet versus buying a put outright, at the cost of capping how much you can make if the stock falls sharply. A day like today — where the headline index was up but specific names (like Tata Group stocks, on the Tata Sons governance rift) were sharply down — is a reminder that this concept is used to express a bearish view on a single stock or sector, not the whole index.
