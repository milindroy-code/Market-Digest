Monday, 16 November's session was a genuine bounce-back — after Friday's (13 Nov) -0.61%/-0.39% close, this morning's gap-up (flagged in today's own Midday Pulse) held all the way into the close. Final numbers: Sensex 82,102.85, up 510.40 points/+0.63% (one PTI-sourced read puts it at 82,104.79/+512.34 points, a minor variance), Nifty 25,291.10, up 146.80 points/+0.58% — intraday high 25,337.60 — cross-confirmed across Business Standard, HDFC Sky, Zee Business and Moneycontrol/TradingView. But the headline gain is an incomplete story: breadth was genuinely weak — only 1,399 stocks advanced on the BSE against 2,807 that declined (169 unchanged) — and total BSE-listed market capitalisation fell by roughly ₹1 lakh crore (from ₹510.18 lakh crore to ₹509.21 lakh crore), despite the index gain. In other words, today's rally was concentrated in a handful of large-cap heavyweights, not the broader market (Nifty Midcap -0.5%, Smallcap -1.1%) — see the Explainer below for why that happens.

The driver was clear: record FII inflows — per NSDL data, FIIs have net bought $8.9 billion of Indian equities so far in November, the highest monthly total since 2002, narrowing calendar-2026's FII outflow to $22.5 billion and trimming the Nifty's 2026 loss to under 3.5%. Crude eased further too — Brent ~$95.40/barrel (-0.4%), with Strait of Hormuz shipments recovering toward pre-war levels and the IEA projecting a 1.7 million barrel-per-day global oil surplus in Q4 2026 — cheaper crude helps the import bill, inflation and the rupee alike. Globally, Asian markets were mixed-to-weak — jitters around Nvidia's leadership (reports of an internal investigation into CEO Jensen Huang, a running story this week) weighed on the AI trade, alongside caution ahead of Nvidia's upcoming earnings and a widely-anticipated Anthropic IPO. India's comparatively low exposure to the crowded AI trade meant those very jitters appeared to redirect capital rotation toward India instead (per HDFC Sky). FMCG led the sector table (Nifty FMCG +1.3% to +1.9%, minor variance across sources), alongside Bank, Infra, Private Bank, Media and PSU Bank (+0.3% to +0.9%).

At the stock level, Tata Motors Passenger Vehicles was today's biggest drag — closing down 8.70% at ₹262.40 — on top of the Q2 consolidated net loss of ₹5,531 crore (on exceptional items) flagged this morning and at midday, a second negative landed today: per HDFC Sky, the company cut its JLR unit's FY27 EBIT margin guidance from 4% to 2-3%. Other Nifty losers included Tata Steel (-2.37%, ₹182.26), Maruti Suzuki (-2.20%, ₹12,571), Hindalco (-2.15%, ₹923.30) and BSE Ltd (-1.28%, ₹3,887.40). On the gainers' side, Max Healthcare (+6.63%, ₹1,009.90), Tata Consumer (+3.57%, ₹1,117.50), ITC (+3.54%, ₹295.10), Apollo Hospitals (+2.87%, ₹8,771) and Nestle India (+2.68%, ₹1,297) led. Midday's open stock stories resolved into the close: Kaynes Technology -13% (Q2 profit down 61.6% YoY, a touch deeper than midday's -11%/-62% read), Belrise Industries +9% (pared from midday's intraday +12% high, despite Q2 PAT up 25% YoY), Deepak Fertilisers +7%+ (Q2 profit up 85% YoY, consistent with midday), and one new name — TVS Srichakra +7% (strong Q2 results). ABB India — whose analyst call was scheduled for 10:30-11:30 AM IST today per an earlier preview — had no actual Q2 FY27 (September-quarter) figures found in any search this run, remaining fully unconfirmed. Sector laggards were Realty (-1.3% to -1.7%), Pharma (-1.5% to -1.7%), Metal (-1.5%), Auto (-1.2% to -1.3%) and a mild Energy (-0.3% to -0.6%). More than 150 stocks touched 52-week highs today — a sign stock-specific strength was also a factor, not just index-level FII buying.

On the macro front, today's biggest surprise was the October WPI release — 12.29%, up sharply from September's 11.39%. Fuel and power was the main driver, with that group's inflation rising from 25.85% to 32.84%, and the crude petroleum/natural gas sub-group alone climbing from 57.32% to 70.80%. Food inflation eased slightly (from 8.66% to 8.58%), while primary articles inflation ticked up (from 8.67% to 9.24%). The April-October FY27 cumulative WPI rate stands at 10.14%; these are provisional figures (based on an 81.1% weighted response rate). Currency and commodities: the rupee broke its recent weakening streak today — ₹93.93/USD, up 31 paise from Friday's ₹94.24 — helped by both the FII inflows and softer crude. Today's MCX gold/silver close — which through midday was showing a sharp crash (gold -1.39%, silver at a record-low -3.75%) on 92% odds of a December Fed rate hike — wasn't confirmed this run; Bank Nifty's exact closing level (only a sector-level +0.3% to +0.9% range is known) and India VIX also remained unconfirmed this run.

Explainer: How The Index Can Rise While The Market Falls

Today the Sensex closed up 0.63% and the Nifty up 0.58% — yet only 1,399 stocks advanced on the BSE against 2,807 that declined, and the market lost roughly ₹1 lakh crore of total capitalisation. That isn't a contradiction; it's the natural result of how the indices are built. The Sensex and Nifty are market-cap-weighted, meaning a handful of large stocks carry most of the weight in their moves — it isn't a 1:1 "average" of every listed company. Today, heavyweights like Max Healthcare, ITC, Tata Consumer, Apollo Hospitals and Nestle saw strong buying, and that alone can pull the index up even while more than 2,800 other stocks fall. That's why traders also watch "market breadth" (the advance-decline ratio) and not just the index level — a positive index paired with negative breadth often signals a narrow rally rather than a broad-based one, and narrow rallies tend to be less durable than the headline number suggests.

Strategy Corner
Today's Concept
Covered Call

If you already hold a stock, sell a call option against it. You collect the premium as income, and if the stock stays below the strike, you keep both the stock and the premium. The tradeoff: if the stock rallies hard past the strike, you miss out on gains beyond it, since you're obligated to sell at that price. Today's pick from the sequential rotation, tagged "any" — a flexible concept that's relevant even in a session like today's narrow, heavyweight-led rally, where you can generate some extra income on existing holdings unless you have strong conviction they'll keep running higher. This is purely an educational concept, not a recommendation for any specific trade.
