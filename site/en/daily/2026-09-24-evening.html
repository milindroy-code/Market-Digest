Macro & Policy
  • IRDAI's insurance-distribution reform proposal — the biggest new driver behind today's selloff. The regulator has proposed commission caps (roughly 5-20% for intermediaries and 6.25-25% for agents on individual products, depending on the premium payment term), tighter Expense of Management (EoM) limits, a "dark pattern" ban on insurer websites (such as requiring consumers to submit personal data before seeing basic price/service information), and a ban on volume-linked incentives and sponsored trips for bank/NBFC employees. This is a consultation paper — feedback is open until 25 October 2026, so the final rules could still change.
  • RBI and SEBI — unchanged: repo rate at 5.25% (next MPC 5–7 October).
  • US-China — the Trump-Xi summit is underway in Washington; no fresh concrete headline yet, only the trade-truce extension already confirmed.

Explainer

Why does a regulator capping its own industry's costs crash stocks? IRDAI's new proposal puts strict limits on the commissions insurers pay distributors, and on their total "Expense of Management" (EoM) — the full overhead of selling and servicing a policy. That's good for consumers: lower commissions mean more of every rupee goes toward actual coverage instead of middlemen. But for the stock market, a large share of insurer and insurtech revenue comes from exactly these high-commission distribution models — for a platform like PB Fintech, commission income is the core business. When a regulator signals it will squeeze those margins overnight, the market immediately revises future earnings estimates downward — which is why a consumer-friendly reform can still trigger a sharp, single-day stock crash, as it did today with PB Fintech's ~30% fall.

Strategy Corner
Today's Concept
Protective Put

Buy a put option on a stock you already own, as insurance. If the stock falls, the put gains value and offsets the loss; if it doesn't, you've paid a small premium for peace of mind — the same logic as paying for car insurance you hope never to use. A session like today's — where a fresh oil shock and a fresh regulatory shock both hit in the same day — is exactly the kind of scenario where investors consider a protective put to hedge downside without fully exiting an existing position.
