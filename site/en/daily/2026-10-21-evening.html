Explainer: Why Rising Crude Oil Rattles The Indian Stock Market

India imports about 85% of its crude oil needs, so when a global benchmark like Brent rises — as it did today on Gulf shipping attacks and the Iran-war stalemate — the effect reaches Indian markets through three channels. First, the import bill grows, widening the current account deficit and pressuring the rupee. Second, costlier crude pushes up petrol, diesel and transportation costs, feeding straight into retail inflation (CPI) and clouding the outlook for RBI rate cuts. Third, oil-sensitive sectors (aviation, paints, tyres, FMCG logistics) see their input costs rise, squeezing margins. That's why a day like today happens — a purely macro trigger (crude) can turn an IT-led gap-up straight into red territory, with no company-specific news involved at all.

Bull Call Spread

Buy a call at a lower strike and sell a call at a higher strike, same expiry. This caps both your cost and your maximum profit compared to buying a call outright, making it a way to express a moderately bullish view more cheaply — you give up unlimited upside in exchange for a lower breakeven.
