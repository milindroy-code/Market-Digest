Today's session (Friday, 6 November) opened even weaker than this morning's own cautious GIFT Nifty signal (futures -168.50 points/-0.68%, implied open ~24,368). In early trade, the Nifty slipped to an intraday low of 24,388.05 (-148.55 points/-0.60%) and the Sensex to 79,016.05 (-294 points/-0.37%) — against Thursday's (5 November) confirmed close of 24,536.60/79,310.01 — a Free Press Journal report (updated 12:23 PM IST) and HDFC Sky's pre-open note cross-confirm this choppy start. But the dip didn't last: the Sensex rebounded more than 400 points within the first 15 minutes of trade, and the Nifty climbed to 24,549 by 9:40 AM. By late morning, both indices are solidly positive — the Sensex at 79,760.10 (+443.87 points/+0.56%) and the Nifty at 24,612.85 (+81 points/+0.33%), reclaiming the 24,600 level (PSU Watch) — a clean V-shaped recovery that shrugs off overnight carnage on Wall Street (Nasdaq -5.52%, its worst day since April 2025) and across Asia (Nikkei -6.2%, Kospi's circuit-breaker-triggering plunge). No circuit filter or surprise policy move has occurred this run — the only "break" is the reversal itself. The most notable divergence: Nifty IT is holding up strongly (up roughly +2% through the morning, per Free Press Journal), even as the very AI/semiconductor stocks it's benchmarked against were at the centre of last night's US rout — HCL Tech, TCS, Infosys and Bharti Airtel are among today's gainers. Mahindra & Mahindra leads the index, up more than 4% after reporting a 25% year-on-year rise in September-quarter profit (PSU Watch); Titan is also up (+2.59%) on Q2 results that beat estimates — finally confirming the results that were still pending as of this morning's edition, though an exact profit figure couldn't be confirmed this run. On the other side, Asian Paints, Power Grid, Bajaj Finance and Bharat Electronics are trading lower, and two more "beat-but-falls" cases have emerged — Britannia Industries is down 4.49% and LIC down 4.02%, both despite stronger quarterly numbers (echoing the SBI pattern flagged earlier this week). Early in the dip, Nifty Metal (-2%), Realty (-1.56%) and Chemicals/Oil & Gas/Consumer Durables/PSU Bank/FMCG/Auto (down to -1.34%) were the weakest; most of that weakness has unwound with the broader rebound. FIIs continue to buy heavily through the global rout — cumulative net purchases of ₹35,781 crore over the past five sessions, including Thursday's ₹14,974.68 crore alone — while DIIs are reportedly booking some profit today, though an exact figure isn't available yet. Crude extended its rebound: Brent is up 2.35% to $100.60/barrel and WTI above $91.90, both firming further on the safe-haven bid that began overnight. Strategy Corner
Today's Concept
The Iron Butterfly

A tighter cousin of the iron condor: sell an at-the-money call and put, and buy further out-of-the-money call and put as protection. It pays out most if the underlying finishes exactly at the middle strike, offering a higher potential reward than a condor for a market expected to stay very still — at the cost of a narrower profitable range. This is today's pick from the sequential rotation, tagged "range-bound" — a mixed fit for a day like today, which was anything but still, with a sharp dip-then-rally instead. This is purely an educational concept, not a recommendation to place the trade.
