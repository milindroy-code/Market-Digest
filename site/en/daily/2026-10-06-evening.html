Explainer

Why did bank stocks rally today despite a looming rate hike? Kotak Mahindra Bank and Axis Bank both released Q2 "business updates" today — operational numbers (deposits, advances, loan-book growth) that a company voluntarily shares ahead of its full quarterly results, so the market doesn't have to wait. These numbers are a company-specific fundamental signal — "how fast is our own business growing" — which is a separate question from the RBI's macro rate decision. That's how, in a single session, banking stocks can rise despite an upcoming rate hike that could squeeze their margins: if the bank's own growth is strong enough, it can outweigh that macro headwind. The market prices these two signals separately — how much business a bank is writing is a different question from how much margin it earns on that business — and today, for Kotak and Axis, the first question dominated the second.

Today's Concept
Max Pain Theory

The idea that an underlying tends to gravitate toward the strike price at which option buyers, collectively, lose the most money by expiry — because option sellers (often larger, better-capitalized players) benefit from that outcome. It's a widely watched but imperfect signal, useful as one input among several rather than a guaranteed prediction. Ahead of tomorrow's binary RBI event, watching options positioning (max pain included) can offer one extra lens on what the market is pricing in. This is an educational concept, not a recommendation to place the trade.
