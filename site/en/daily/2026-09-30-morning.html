Explainer

Why is Nifty testing its 200-week moving average such a big deal? A "moving average" simply tracks an index's average closing level over its last X sessions — the 200-week version, roughly the average over the last three and a half years. As long as an index trades above that level, its long-term trend is considered healthy; when it decisively breaks below, technical analysts treat it as a structural warning sign, not just a short-term dip. That's why Tuesday's event drew so much attention: Nifty tested its 200-week MA (~22,600) for the first time since the March 2020 Covid crash — meaning neither the 2022 bear phase nor any correction since had ever pulled the market back down to this level. HDFC Securities' Nagaraj Shetti views any bounce toward 22,900-23,000 as a "sell-on-rise" opportunity, while SBI Securities' Sudeep Shah flags 22,550-22,570 as the next immediate support — with a risk of a slide to 22,400 if that breaks too. The concrete number: Nifty just posted its worst September F&O series in 25 years, down ~6.7% for the series.

Today's Concept
Max Pain Theory

The idea that an underlying tends to gravitate toward the strike price at which option buyers, collectively, lose the most money by expiry — because option sellers (often larger, better-capitalized players) benefit from that outcome. It's a widely watched but imperfect signal, useful as one input among several rather than a guaranteed prediction. Tuesday's session showed this in action — max pain shifted from 22,600 to 22,750 as options positioning repriced through the closing hour, and Nifty's final close (22,716.20) landed remarkably close to that new max-pain level. With the market still searching for direction after its 200-week MA test, today's max-pain level is worth watching as a reference point — not a trading signal, but an input on where the options market currently expects things to settle.
