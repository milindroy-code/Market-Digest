Explainer

What does a Grey Market Premium sitting flat at ₹0 actually mean? The Grey Market Premium (GMP) is an unofficial, off-exchange price at which traders commit to buy or sell IPO shares before allotment — it gives retail investors a rough sense of what premium (or discount) a stock might open at on listing day. When GMP goes flat at ₹0 — as it has for both Varmora Granito and Anand Seamless today — it means grey market participants aren't expecting a meaningful listing-day gain, but they aren't pricing in a big loss either. It also lines up with weak subscription numbers (Varmora's at just 0.27x): when demand is tepid, the market tends to price the listing roughly around the issue price. GMP is an unofficial, unregulated indicator — and it's sometimes wrong — but it's a useful, quick directional read on how an IPO is being received, without waiting for allotment.

Strategy Corner
Today's Concept
Bull Call Spread

Buy a call at a lower strike and sell a call at a higher strike, same expiry. This caps both your cost and your maximum profit compared to buying a call outright, making it a way to express a moderately bullish view more cheaply — you give up unlimited upside in exchange for a lower breakeven. Today Nifty's daily RSI sits at ~31 — near-oversold — and the US-Iran phased Hormuz-deal talks could open the door to a relief-bounce scenario. If such a bounce materializes, a Bull Call Spread is a cost-efficient way to position for a moderate recovery move, without needing the whole selloff to be erased.
