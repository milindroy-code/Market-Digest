Today's session (Wednesday, 4 November) opened higher, wrong-footing this morning's flat-to-negative GIFT Nifty signal (~23,728-23,732). By around 9:30 AM, the Nifty was trading at 23,921.20 (+110.35pts/+0.46%) and the Sensex at 77,301.59 (+383.74pts/+0.50%), against yesterday's (Tuesday, 3 November) confirmed close of 23,810.85/76,917.85 — both HDFC Sky and TradeBrains cross-confirm this early gain. But those morning gains fully reversed by midday: by roughly 12:00-12:24 PM IST, the Nifty had slipped into the 23,753-23,771 range (-0.16% to -0.24%) and the Sensex into the 76,731-76,784 range (-0.17% to -0.24%) — two independent sources (Business Standard's live blog, TradeBrains' live update) both cross-confirm this midday reversal, with small exact-number differences attributable to slightly different timestamps. No circuit filter or surprise policy move has occurred, but this is a genuine — if mild — intraday reversal: the entire morning gain had evaporated by midday. By sector, Nifty Realty led throughout the session (+1.76% to +2.10%), FMCG also opened strong (+1.42-2.5%), while Nifty IT was the weakest (falling as much as -2.43% by midday) and PSU Bank stayed under pressure too; banking stocks overall held up "relatively resilient." On the stock-specific front, Equitas Small Finance Bank jumped as much as +8% after naming Balaji Nuthalapadi interim CEO (subject to RBI approval) — notable since yesterday's edition flagged its sixth straight quarterly loss. Two divergences also stood out: GE Vernova T&D India fell -3.2% despite a 6 GW HVDC order from Power Grid, and SKF India fell as much as -6% despite raising its FY27 revenue-growth guidance above 20% — the same beat-but-stock-falls pattern seen with SBI earlier this week. In commodities, MCX gold (December futures) rose +2.07% to ₹1,42,335/10g and silver +3.42% to ₹1,85,849/kg — a sharp intraday jump in both. Brent crude's exact level conflicts across sources (~$102.71/barrel, -0.86%, per one source; ~$104.04/barrel, +0.43%, per another) — both are flagged rather than reconciled. USD/INR is around ₹94.75. India VIX ticked up from yesterday's 15.72 to ~16.0-16.06. Asia stayed weak today too — the Nikkei fell 3.2-4.2% to a three-month low, extending the Asia weakness flagged in yesterday's edition. No fresh FII/DII figure for today (4 November) was confirmed this run — yesterday's figure (FII net buy ₹6,836.81cr, DII net sell ₹3,678.85cr) is carried forward. On the corporate front, NSE's (profit +23% YoY) and MCX's (profit +131% YoY to ~₹455cr) Q2 results remain confirmed (carried from yesterday's edition); BHEL confirmed orders from Adani Power for four 2,800 MW projects; the RBI set up a new Technical Consultative Committee for Financial Markets. The single biggest background trigger is still the US midterm elections, whose clearer result remains pending.

Strategy Corner
Today's Concept
Bear Put Spread

The mirror image of a bull call spread: buy a put at a higher strike, sell a put at a lower strike. It reduces the cost of a bearish bet versus buying a put outright, at the cost of capping how much you can make if the stock falls sharply. This is today's pick from the sequential rotation, tagged "downtrend" — but today's fit is only partial: this is a mild midday reversal, not a clean downtrend, so the concept is better read in the context of a cautious, wait-and-watch session than as a strong directional call. This is purely an educational concept, not a recommendation to place the trade.
