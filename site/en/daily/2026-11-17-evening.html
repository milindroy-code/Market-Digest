Explainer: Why The Same Results Get Called Both "Q2" And "Q3"

Today, ABB India's Jul-Sep quarter results were called "Q2 FY27" by some sources and "Q3 FY27" by others — both are technically correct, just using different reference frames. Most Indian companies run a financial year starting 1 April, so their Q1 = Apr-Jun, Q2 = Jul-Sep, Q3 = Oct-Dec — by that reckoning, Jul-Sep results are "Q2 FY27." But some analysts and platforms use the calendar-quarter convention instead (Jan-Mar = Q1, Apr-Jun = Q2, Jul-Sep = Q3), which makes the very same results "Q3 FY27." This isn't just a quirky label mismatch — if you're comparing a stock's YoY or QoQ numbers and two sources' quarter-labels don't line up, you can end up comparing two entirely different quarters by mistake (as happened today with ABB India's ₹362cr and ₹442.52cr figures, which turned out to be two different quarters, not two readings of the same one). The safe habit is to always check the exact date range ("July-September 2026") rather than trusting the quarter label alone.

Today's Concept
Bear Put Spread

The mirror image of a bull call spread: buy a put at a higher strike, sell a put at a lower strike. It reduces the cost of a bearish bet versus buying a put outright, at the cost of capping how much you can make if the stock falls sharply. Today's pick in the sequential rotation, tagged "downtrend" — but today's session closed green (Sensex +0.52%, Nifty +0.38%), just a fade of the midday gains rather than an outright downtrend — an acknowledged mismatch. This is purely an educational concept, not a recommendation to place the trade.
