Explainer

What exactly are LTCG tax and STT, and why can a finance minister's "we're listening" comment move markets this much? LTCG (Long-Term Capital Gains) tax is what you pay on profit from equities held over a year — currently 12.5% (hiked from 10% in Budget 2024). STT (Securities Transaction Tax) is charged on every trade, win or lose, adding a cost at every layer of trading. Together they set the "friction cost" of investing in equities. Markets are forward-looking enough that they don't wait for a confirmed policy change — even a senior minister's "we're open to it" signal shifts expectations about future cost-of-capital, because if LTCG or STT comes down, equities become relatively more attractive to every investor, starting today. That's why today's move came from a speculative comment, not an actual Budget announcement.

Today's Concept
Bull Call Spread

A Bull Call Spread involves buying a call at a lower strike and selling a call at a higher strike, same expiry. This caps both your cost and your maximum profit compared to buying a call outright — you give up unlimited upside in exchange for a lower breakeven. It's a way to express a moderately bullish view more cheaply — fitting for a day like today, where Nifty is in a clear trend and pushing toward a specific resistance level (22,747), but whether it actually clears that level remains uncertain. This is an educational concept only, not a recommendation to place the trade.
