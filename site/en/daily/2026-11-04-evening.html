Explainer: What Is Sector Rotation?

Today is a textbook example of sector rotation: within the same broad market rally, Nifty Media jumped over 5% while Nifty IT was the only sector to close in the red. Sector rotation happens when institutional money shifts out of one sector and into another — without the overall market necessarily changing direction. Today's move out of IT was partly driven by fresh global concern over AI valuations and AI capex (also visible in tech-heavy Asian indices like Kospi and Taiex), which dragged down domestic IT majors (TCS, HCL Tech, Infosys) even though their own India-specific business isn't directly tied to that cycle. That same money then flowed into Media, Realty and Metal — sectors that had lagged this rally so far and where valuations looked relatively cheap. That's why a single day's index number ("Nifty +0.63%") doesn't tell the whole story — which sectors rose and which fell shows where money is actually moving within the market.

Strategy Corner
Today's Concept
Calendar Spread

Sell a near-term option and buy a longer-dated option at the same strike. The near-term option decays faster (time decay accelerates as expiry approaches), so if the stock stays roughly flat, the position can profit from that difference in decay rates — a strategy built around time itself, not direction. With a "range-bound" tag, this is today's sequential-rotation pick — a mixed fit, since today's closing move was itself a clear uptrend continuation (a fifth straight green session), even though the day's own intraday whipsaw (lower open, a midday reversal, then a rally into the close) was a genuine two-way, range-bound chop within the session. This is an educational concept only, not a recommendation for any specific trade.
