Explainer: Kospi's "Sidecar" Halt — When a Rally Pauses Itself

This morning, South Korea's Kospi index staged such a sharp rebound (after two days of AI-funding-concerns selloff) that it triggered a "sidecar" — an automatic circuit-breaker that temporarily pauses program trading (algorithmic, basket-based buy/sell orders), not the whole market. When a broad-market futures contract (like the KOSPI 200) moves beyond a threshold (typically around 5%) and holds that move for a few minutes, the exchange automatically puts program-trade orders on hold for a short window (usually five minutes). The idea is to let large algorithmic orders — which can amplify volatility purely through their own speed — pause for a moment, giving the market a brief chance to catch its breath on price discovery, without halting trading altogether. It's different from a full circuit breaker, which shuts down the entire market during a major crash — a sidecar only slows one type of order, and can trigger more than once in a single session if volatility stays extreme. Today's trigger came on the upside, during a sharp rally, not a crash — a useful reminder that the mechanism is direction-neutral, targeting speed and magnitude rather than a specific direction.

Strategy Corner
Today's Concept
Covered Call

If you already hold a stock, sell a call option against it. You collect the premium as income, and if the stock stays below the strike, you keep both the stock and the premium. The tradeoff: if the stock rallies hard past the strike, you miss out on gains beyond it, since you're obligated to sell at that price. A week as event-heavy as this one — Jio's mega-IPO subscription opening, Infosys' Q2 results, and the question of follow-through in bank stocks, all at once — is exactly the kind of setup where investors looking for a bit of extra income on existing holdings, without taking on new directional risk, discuss a strategy like the covered call. This is purely an educational concept, not a recommendation to place the trade.
