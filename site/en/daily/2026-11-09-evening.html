Explainer: What Does A Lock-In Period Expiry Actually Mean?

When a company goes public, SEBI rules bar its promoters, anchor investors and certain pre-IPO shareholders from selling their shares for a fixed period — typically 6, 12 or 18 months — known as the lock-in period. The idea is to stop insiders from dumping their holdings right after listing and to give retail investors confidence that those with the most information genuinely have skin in the game for the long haul. But when that lock-in expires, all those shares suddenly become freely tradable — a sudden increase in supply that can pressure the stock if large holders choose to cash out (though that's never guaranteed — many don't sell immediately). Today's NSE data point shows this concept at scale — over the coming months, through 26 February 2027, lock-ins will expire at 116 companies, potentially bringing roughly $35 billion worth of shares into supply. For a careful investor, this is a useful calendar-based risk to watch — the odds of volatility around a specific stock's lock-in expiry date rise, even if nothing about the company's underlying fundamentals has changed.

Strategy Corner
Today's Concept
Iron Condor

An Iron Condor involves selling an out-of-the-money call spread and an out-of-the-money put spread at the same time. You collect premium upfront and keep it if the underlying stays within a range through expiry — a trade that profits from a market going nowhere. Risk is capped on both sides, which is why it's a popular income strategy in sideways markets. It's a sequential pick tagged "range-bound," and today's session genuinely resonates with that theme — the Nifty covered roughly 245 points of intraday range (25,084 high to 24,838 low) only to close back near the flat-to-marginally-positive level where the day began, with a net move of just +0.23%. For a disciplined range-bound trader, exactly this "volatile intraday, flat net" pattern is the setup where a strategy like the Iron Condor gets considered. This is purely an educational concept, not a recommendation on any specific trade or strike selection.
