Explainer

What actually is a "resistance breakout," and why doesn't one close confirm it? Resistance is a price level where selling pressure has repeatedly capped a rally — like Nifty's 22,747/22,947 zone, which had kept the index from advancing further for weeks. When price clears that zone on strong volume, it's called a "breakout," and classic technical theory holds that a broken resistance level often becomes the new support — the old ceiling turns into the new floor. But a single session's close doesn't "confirm" this: traders wait for a retest, where price comes back to that level and bounces off it (acting as support), rather than slipping back inside the old zone. If that retest fails, it's called a "false breakout" or "fakeout" — a very common trap. Today's close is a genuinely bullish signal, but it's the next session or two of price action that will actually confirm whether this breakout holds.

Today's Concept
Bull Call Spread

Buy an in-the-money (or at-the-money) call and simultaneously sell a higher-strike, out-of-the-money call with the same expiry. The premium from the sold call partially offsets the cost of the bought call, making the trade cheaper than a naked long call — but profit is also capped above the sold strike. It's a classic "moderately bullish" structure: for when you expect the underlying to rise, but only up to a specific level — exactly the setup today's resistance breakout presents, where the next target could itself become a new resistance level rather than unlimited upside. This is an educational concept, not a recommendation to place the trade.
