Explainer

Why does the US 10-year Treasury yield falling below 5% push the Nasdaq to a record — and why do AI stocks move the most? A bond yield is essentially the "discount rate" investors use to work out today's value of a company's future earnings. When yields fall, the present value of profits expected years from now goes up — and that effect is strongest for companies whose valuation is mostly built on distant future growth rather than today's earnings. AI and chip stocks sit squarely in that bucket: their valuations are largely a bet on how big AI-driven demand will be years from now, so when yields fall, their prices move the most — exactly what showed up today in Intel's, AMD's and Arm's double-digit percentage gains. That's why falling oil, falling yields and AI optimism combined on a single day to push the Nasdaq to a fresh record, even though last week's Fed hike was still, technically, a hawkish one.

Strategy Corner
Concept of the day
Averaging Down vs. Pyramiding

Averaging down means buying more of a losing position to lower your average cost — risky, since you're adding to a trade that's already going against you. Pyramiding is the opposite: adding to a winning position as it moves in your favor. Most experienced traders favor pyramiding into strength over averaging into weakness, since it means the market is confirming the thesis rather than you fighting it. On a day when Wall Street's overnight rally could set up a fresh test of resistance, that discipline matters especially.
