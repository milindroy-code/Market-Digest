Explainer: Why FOMC Minutes Can Move Markets Even Though The Rate Decision Is Already Known

When the Fed meets and decides on rates, that's immediate news. But three weeks later, the Fed releases detailed "minutes" — a summary of what individual policymakers actually said in the room. These often carry nuance the initial statement didn't: how close the vote was, which officials pushed for more hikes, what data they're watching. When the minutes turn out more hawkish than the market had priced in — as happened with October's minutes, where "almost all officials" favoured further hikes — it shifts the probability investors assign to future moves, via tools like CME's FedWatch. For emerging markets like India, that matters doubly: higher US rates pull capital back toward dollar assets (a risk of FII outflows) and keep the dollar strong against the rupee. So even without a single actual rate move, a shift in the odds alone is enough to ripple through GIFT Nifty and global risk sentiment.

Today's Concept
Trailing Stop-Loss

Instead of a fixed stop-loss, a trailing stop moves up as the price rises — locking in gains while still giving the trade room to run. It's a way to implement the trading wisdom of "let your winners run," without needing to predict exactly when the trend will end. Today's pick from the sequential rotation, tagged "uptrend" — a fit with November's overall rally of more than 10%: for investors who've already booked gains in that rally, this is exactly the right concept to protect those gains now, as yesterday's and today's pullback signals build, without exiting the position entirely. This is purely an educational concept, not a recommendation to place the trade.
