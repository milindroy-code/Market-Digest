The stabilization seen through midday didn't last into the close — by ~11 AM, Sensex and Nifty were down just 0.06%/0.24% (72,438.08/22,565.10), but a sharp, high-volatility selloff in the afternoon turned the whole session around: Sensex plunged ~1,280 points from the day's high of ~72,573 to a fresh 2026 intraday low of ~71,293, while Nifty slipped below 22,200. The market pared back a few hundred points off the lows before the close, but the damage was done — Nifty settled at 22,421.95, down 198.50 points (0.88%) from Wednesday's (30 Sept) 22,620.45, and Sensex closed at 71,909.70, down 570.59 points (0.79%) — a fourth straight red session for both indices. Today's trigger wasn't geopolitics but the bond market: the US 10-year Treasury yield climbed to ~5.30% and the 30-year hit a 24-year high (~5.64%) — this even as Tuesday night's core PCE inflation print (the Fed's preferred gauge) actually came in cooler than expected (3% year-on-year), only for strong US corporate earnings and a GDP upgrade to overshadow that good inflation news. The result was an unusual divergence: October Fed rate-hike odds fell from ~51% to ~38%, yet long-end yields still rose — a sign markets are now worrying more about growth/supply than about hikes. This widening yield gap is pulling FII money out of Indian equities and into dollar-safe US Treasuries, on top of September's already-heavy ₹45,536 crore net outflow. The sector split sharpened further today: Nifty IT rose 2.1% (Infosys +4.11% to ₹1,035, today's Nifty50 top gainer, ahead of TCS's and HCL Tech's upcoming Q2 results), while Nifty Auto cracked ~3.5% (Bajaj Auto -7.62% to ₹10,045, Maruti Suzuki -4.86% to ₹11,386) and Capital Goods was also caught in a broad sell-off. The rupee closed at ₹96.11/dollar (down 14 paise), and India VIX swung sharply through the session — from an early print near 13.7 to a spike above 15 as the selloff deepened, before settling closer to ~14. Markets will be closed tomorrow (2 October) for Gandhi Jayanti — the next session is Monday, 5 October, when RBI's three-day MPC meeting also begins and Orient Cables India, German Green Steel & Power and AceVector all list together.

Explainer

Today's most confusing question: if inflation (core PCE) actually came in cooler, why did US bond yields rise? Yields aren't just a reflection of the Fed's overnight rate — they also capture investors' expectations about future growth, government borrowing/supply, and the "term premium" (extra return demanded for the risk of holding long-dated bonds). When corporate earnings and GDP data beat expectations, investors start expecting the economy to run hot for longer, so they demand a higher yield to hold long bonds — even as near-term Fed-hike odds fall on the good inflation news. That's exactly why October hike odds dropped from 51% to 38% today, yet the 10-year and 30-year yields still climbed. The direct effect of higher US yields is that risk-free US Treasuries become relatively more attractive than emerging-market equities like India's — so FIIs pull money out, pressuring both the rupee and Indian stocks. That's the mechanism behind today's crash-then-partial-recovery session.

Strategy Corner
Today's Concept
Protective Put

Buy a put option on a stock you already own, as insurance. If the stock falls, the put gains value and offsets the loss; if it doesn't, you've paid a small premium for peace of mind — the same logic as paying for car insurance you hope never to use. A session like today — where midday's stabilization turned into a sharp ~1,280-point intraday crash by the afternoon — is exactly the kind of market where traders explore protecting existing holdings through an uncertain stretch (like the next MPC meeting or this week's US data) without fully exiting, a concept that's educational, not a recommendation to place the trade.
