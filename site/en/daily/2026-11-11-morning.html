Explainer: Wall Street Fell, So Why Is GIFT Nifty Up?

This morning's most counter-intuitive signal: overnight, all three major US indices closed red (S&P -0.38%, Nasdaq -0.74%) on hot CPI data and Nvidia's -5.5% drop, yet GIFT Nifty signals a ~+1.1% to +1.2% gap-up against Monday's Nifty close — a genuine ~150-basis-point swing between the two moves. That's possible because a market's move is never purely "global sentiment" — it's built from specific triggers. Last night's US selloff was driven by two largely US-specific things: a hot inflation print that mainly affects the Fed's own rate path, and Nvidia's own company-specific CEO-investigation issue. India's local tailwinds are different: crude has been cooling for three straight days (Brent ~$91.85), India VIX itself is falling (17.42), and FIIs have been net buyers since last week (~₹45,960cr). When one market's own domestic setup is supportive enough, it doesn't automatically "import" another market's sentiment-driven selloff — this is exactly the mechanism that occasionally breaks index correlation, and it's why reading "Wall Street fell" alone as bearish for India can be the wrong read.

Strategy Corner
Today's Concept
Bear Put Spread

Buy a put at a higher strike and sell a put at the same expiry at a lower strike — the mirror image of a bull call spread. The premium collected from the short put lowers your net cost versus buying a put outright, at the cost of capping your maximum profit to the spread's width. This is today's sequential rotation pick, and honestly, its "downtrend" tag doesn't directly match today's ~+1.1-1.2% gap-up setup — but there's a genuinely interesting fit anyway: a trader who believes today's gap-up isn't sustainable against the Fed's newly hawkish repricing could look at exactly this structure, as a measured hedge rather than the larger premium outlay of a naked put. This is purely an educational concept, not a recommendation for any specific trade or strike selection.
