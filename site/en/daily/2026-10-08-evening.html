Explainer

Why does an IPO's subscription number jump so dramatically on the final day? Take Vishal Nirmiti: mid-week, overall subscription was running at just ~0.57-0.73x, but by the time bidding closed it had reached 1.71x. The reason lies in how the three investor categories behave differently. Retail and NII (non-institutional) investors tend to place most of their bids in the first two or three days, often watching how demand builds. QIBs (Qualified Institutional Buyers) — mutual funds, insurance companies, FIIs — deliberately hold back their bulk bidding until the final day, often the last hour or two, because they want to see how retail and NII demand has shaped up first and minimize their own pricing risk. That's why the QIB category alone tends to move the most in a subscription window's final hours — in Vishal Nirmiti's case, QIB demand reached 1.27x, a sign of healthy institutional interest even though early retail/NII demand looked muted.

Today's Concept
Bull Call Spread

Buy a call at a lower strike and sell a call at a higher strike, same expiry. This caps both your cost and your maximum profit compared to buying a call outright, making it a way to express a moderately bullish view more cheaply — you give up unlimited upside for a lower breakeven. With yesterday's breakout (Nifty clearing its 22,747/22,947 resistance zone) still unconfirmed and the market in wait-and-watch mode ahead of TCS's results, this is a fitting structure for traders bullish on the breakout continuing while keeping capital at risk limited. This is an educational concept, not a recommendation to place the trade.
