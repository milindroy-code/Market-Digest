Explainer

Tata Trusts called the 17 September board resolution a "legal nullity" or "void ab initio" — what does that actually mean? Under the Companies Act, a company's Articles of Association (AoA) act as its internal rulebook — they decide how board resolutions are validly passed, and how much director approval a given matter needs. When a party says a resolution is "void ab initio," it's arguing the resolution was legally invalid from the very start — as if it never happened at all — because the process by which it passed didn't follow the company's own AoA. That's different from "voidable," where a resolution stays valid until a court formally cancels it. Tata Trusts' argument is that its two nominated directors split 1-1 on Chandrasekaran's reappointment, which it says triggers a specific quorum/consent clause in the AoA — if that holds up, the resolution technically never came into existence, even though the board has been acting on it since. That's exactly why both sides are now preparing to go to the NCLT — this isn't just a governance dispute, but a fundamental legal question of whether that resolution was ever valid at all.

Strategy Corner
Concept of the day
Max Pain Theory

The idea that an underlying tends to gravitate toward the strike price at which option buyers, collectively, lose the most money by expiry — because option sellers (often larger, better-capitalized players) benefit from that outcome. It's a widely watched but imperfect signal, useful as one input among several rather than a guaranteed prediction. Today's fresh weekly cycle is showing its own max pain right around 23,350 — almost exactly where Nifty closed on Tuesday (23,329) — suggesting the options market is still pricing in a fairly range-bound outcome.
