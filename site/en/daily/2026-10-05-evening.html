Explainer

Why does an RBI rate decision move the stock market? When the RBI raises the repo rate, it becomes more expensive for banks to borrow from the RBI — a cost that gets passed on to companies and consumers, pushing up EMIs and making loans pricier. For companies, higher borrowing costs mean lower future profits — and stock prices are largely the present value of a company's future cash flows, so higher rates translate into lower valuations, especially in rate-sensitive sectors like banks, NBFCs, auto and realty. Higher rates also make fixed-income instruments (bonds, FDs) relatively more attractive versus equities, which can pull money out of stocks. That's why this week's RBI decision is being watched so closely — a Reuters poll shows 62% of economists (38 of 61) expecting a 25bp hike, which would be the RBI's first since February 2023. If it happens, the market's reaction will depend less on the number itself and more on how seriously the RBI signals it's prioritising inflation-fighting over growth support.

Today's Concept
Averaging Down vs. Pyramiding

Averaging down means buying more of a losing position to lower your average cost — risky, since you're adding to a trade that's already going against you. Pyramiding is the opposite: adding to a winning position as it moves in your favor. Most experienced traders favor pyramiding into strength over averaging into weakness, since it means the market is confirming the thesis rather than you fighting it. This is an educational concept, not a recommendation to place the trade.
