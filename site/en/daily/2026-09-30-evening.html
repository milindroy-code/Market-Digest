Explainer

Today's quietest but most structurally important event was NSE's twice-a-year Nifty 50 index reshuffle, in which BSE Ltd replaced Wipro. This is a routine process called "reconstitution" — the exchange periodically checks which companies still meet the index's eligibility criteria (market cap, liquidity, free float) and swaps out those that no longer do. It's different from "rebalancing," which only adjusts existing members' weights without changing membership. The big practical effect of reconstitution is that trillions of rupees' worth of index funds and ETFs that passively track the Nifty 50 are forced to adjust their portfolios: buying the new member (BSE), selling the removed one (Wipro), exactly matching the new index weights. That's why such announcement dates see closing-auction volumes many times their normal levels — as happened again today. Interestingly, BSE Ltd itself closed red on its own "index-entry day," a reminder that being added to an index doesn't automatically push a stock's price up.

Strategy Corner
Today's Concept
Long Strangle

Like a straddle, but you buy a call and a put at different (out-of-the-money) strikes instead of the same one. It's cheaper to put on than a straddle because both legs are further from the money, but the underlying needs to move even further before you profit. A lower-cost way to bet on a big move without picking a direction. Today's session — where the sharp midday recovery reversed in the final hour, and Nifty tested its 200-week MA for a second straight day — is exactly the kind of volatility where traders reach for directionless big-move bets.
