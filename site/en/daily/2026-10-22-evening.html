Today's session brought a third straight red close. In line with a negative GIFT Nifty signal, the Sensex gapped down roughly 366-525 points at the open, traded range-bound near Nifty 22,700 through much of the day (stabilizing near Sensex 73,491/Nifty 22,700 by midday), but selling picked up again into the close. The Sensex fell -460.30 pts (-0.62%) to close at 73,249.39, and the Nifty dropped -123.70 pts (-0.54%) to 22,687.65 (against Wednesday's, 21 October's, confirmed close of 73,709.69/22,811.35) — a third straight weak trading session (19, 21 and 22 October, with a Dussehra holiday on the 20th in between). Today also carries a notable technical development: the Nifty's close now sits below the 22,747/22,947 breakout zone it decisively cleared on 15-16 October — the first close back below that zone since the breakout. The real driver behind it remains crude oil: Brent is holding near ~$110-112/barrel, on fresh reports that the US and Israel are weighing renewed strikes on Iran after the 3 November US midterm elections — the same geopolitical risk premium that has pressured markets for over a week. Market breadth stayed negative — 36 of the Nifty's 50 constituents closed in the red — and the broader market fell even harder than the headline indices (Nifty Midcap 100 -0.87%, Smallcap 100 -1.17%), showing the selling wasn't confined to the top 50. Top gainers were Bajaj Finance (+1.74% to ₹942.10), Cipla and UltraTech Cement, while Nestle India (-2.78%) was the biggest loser, alongside Eternal, HDFC Life, HDFC Bank (-1.87% to ₹693.70) and Reliance Industries — a broad-based session that didn't spare defensives either. On the corporate front, Jio Platforms' record ₹30,213 crore mega-IPO had a strong Day 2 — the retail portion got fully subscribed for the first time, QIB demand climbed to ~9.4-9.9x, and overall subscription was reported between 3.5x and 4x (sources vary slightly); the issue closes tomorrow, 23 October.

Explainer: Why Midterm-Timed Geopolitical Risk Moves Crude Oil

On 9 October, Brent eased to around $103/barrel after Trump said the US wouldn't strike Iran before the 3 November midterm elections — markets read that "election timing" as a temporary de-escalation signal. But fresh reports now suggest the US and Israel are weighing renewed strikes after the midterms, which has pushed Brent back up to ~$110-112/barrel — a roughly $7-8/barrel jump in just two weeks. India imports about 85% of its crude needs, so a $7-8/barrel move translates to roughly ~$35-40 million a day in extra import costs (an illustrative estimate, based on India's ~4.5-5 million barrels/day of imports) — which widens the current account deficit, pressures the rupee and pushes up retail inflation through petrol and diesel prices. That's why geopolitical headlines about events "a few weeks away" can still move markets today — the risk premium prices in the possibility of a future event, not just its occurrence.

Today's Concept
Position Sizing

Not every trade deserves the same amount of capital. Professional traders size a position based on how far their stop-loss is and how much of their total capital they're willing to risk on one idea — often just 1-2% per trade — rather than a fixed number of shares or lots. The strategy matters less than the size; a great setup with an oversized position can still wipe out an account.
