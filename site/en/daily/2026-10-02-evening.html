Explainer

What exactly is GMP — grey market premium — and how seriously should you take it? GMP is the unofficial, unregulated price at which an IPO's shares trade in an informal ("grey") market before listing — essentially the market's guess at how much higher or lower the stock will open on listing day. It captures pure demand-supply sentiment: more demand, higher GMP. But it has three big limitations: first, it's an illegal/unregulated market with no official settlement; second, GMP keeps shifting right up to listing day, sometimes sharply; and third, it's swayed by overall market mood (like this week's weak sentiment), not just a company's fundamentals. That's exactly why Orient Cables' strong ₹107 GMP and AceVector's near-zero GMP can coexist — two different businesses, two different investor perceptions, both of which remain only an indicative signal, not a guarantee, until the actual listing on 5 October.

Strategy Corner
Today's Concept
Calendar Spread

Sell a near-term option and buy a longer-dated option at the same strike. The near-term option decays faster (time decay accelerates as expiry approaches), so if the stock stays roughly flat, the position can profit from that difference in decay rates — a strategy built around time itself, not direction. A quiet stretch like a market holiday — with no fresh directional trigger — is exactly the kind of setup where traders explore range-bound, time-based strategies like this one.
