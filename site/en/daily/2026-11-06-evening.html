Today (Friday, 6 November), on Dhanteras, Indian markets shrugged off the biggest global sell-off of the week entirely. GIFT Nifty had signalled a weak start this morning (-0.68%), and the market wrong-footed even that signal by first gapping down to an intraday low of 24,388.05 (Nifty -0.60%, as flagged in today's own midday edition) — but a sharp V-shaped recovery kicked in within the first 15 minutes, and the market kept building momentum all session, genuinely accelerating into the close. The Sensex closed up 1,718.01 points (+2.17%) at 81,028.02 and the Nifty up 493.75 points (+2.01%) at 25,030.35 — against Thursday's (5 November) confirmed close of 79,310.01/24,536.60 — these figures are cross-confirmed by four independent sources: Business Standard's live blog, DSIJ's closing-bell report, ETV Bharat/IANS, and Chinimandi. This is a seventh straight green session, and the Nifty has crossed 25,000 for the first time since 27 February 2026. Over just the past six sessions, the Sensex has gained 6,063.98 points (+8.27%) and the Nifty 1,837.75 points (+8%) (ETV Bharat/IANS) — a genuinely sharp rally. But the most interesting thing about today's close is that the rally was far less broad-based than the previous sessions: market breadth was actually negative — of 3,706 stocks traded on the NSE, 1,439 advanced versus 2,141 declining (126 unchanged), with 80 fresh 52-week highs against 63 lows, and 115 upper circuits versus 121 lower circuits (DSIJ, single-sourced for this specific breadth figure). The broader market was also mildly in the red — Nifty Midcap 100 and Smallcap 100 both closed marginally negative (minor cross-source variance: Midcap 100 -0.02% to -0.54%, Smallcap 100 -0.82% to -0.87%). In other words, today's 2%+ index-level move was genuinely driven by a handful of heavyweight large-caps — particularly banking and financial services — rather than a market-wide rally. Sector-wise, 10 of 12 sectoral indices closed green: Financial Services (+2.57%) and Bank (+2.27%) were the clear leaders, with Pharma (+1.21%), Auto (+0.76%), FMCG (+0.67%), Metal (+0.66%), PSU Bank (+0.60%), IT (+0.43%) and Realty (+0.09%) also higher; only Media (-1.52%) and Energy (-0.05%) closed red. The Nifty 50's biggest index-point contributors were ICICI Bank (+112.07 points), HDFC Bank (+95.09 points) and Bharti Airtel (+34.36 points) — these three alone drove most of the index's gain; the drags were tiny too (Eicher Motors -1.32, Power Grid -1.24, Shriram Finance -0.88 points), confirming there was no genuine sell-off anywhere, just narrow participation at the top. At the stock level, Mahindra & Mahindra (+5.11%) was the day's biggest gainer (after a strong move the previous day too), on the back of its Q2 FY27 results — net profit up 25% YoY to ₹4,588 crore and revenue up 25% to ₹57,553 crore (Business Standard). ICICI Bank, Titan, HDFC Bank, Axis Bank, Adani Ports, Sun Pharma, Tata Steel, NTPC, Bajaj Finance and Kotak Bank were also among the top gainers (ETV Bharat/Chinimandi; exact percentage moves for each weren't found this run); Power Grid, Eicher Motors and Grasim were the top losers — though their drag was minor too. A big batch of fresh Q2 FY27 corporate results landed today: Bharti Airtel's net profit rose 31% YoY to ₹8,888 crore (revenue +17% to ₹60,878 crore) — per the company's own quarterly calendar, these results were due on 5 November (Business Standard, single-sourced for this exact figure). Titan Company's results, which had been flagged as pending for the past two runs, were finally confirmed with a profit figure today: +114% YoY to ₹2,393 crore (Business Standard, single-sourced; the stock itself rose +2.95% intraday). ONGC's PAT rose 47% to ₹18,535 crore, alongside an interim dividend of ₹8/share. Britannia Industries' profit rose 11% to ₹726 crore. Two results came in lower: Glenmark Pharmaceuticals (-56% to ₹264 crore) and Godfrey Phillips India (-37% to ₹190 crore, on higher cigarette taxation). Paras Healthcare reported its first results since listing — a profit of ₹40.35 crore. Explainer: The Index Is Up 2%, But More Stocks Fell Than Rose — How?

Today's data looks like a paradox: the Nifty and Sensex both rose 2%+, yet 2,141 stocks fell on the NSE against just 1,439 that rose — meaning more stocks ended the day red than green, even as the index closed firmly green. The reason is market-cap weighting. The Nifty and Sensex aren't "equal-weighted" — each stock's impact on the index is proportional to its total market capitalisation. That's why three heavyweight stocks like ICICI Bank, HDFC Bank and Bharti Airtel can, between them (240+ index points combined today), single-handedly pull the whole index higher, regardless of what the other 2,000-plus smaller stocks are doing. It's a useful reminder that reading the Nifty number alone as "the whole market is up" can mislead — getting the real picture needs a look at breadth (advances vs declines) and the broader indices (Midcap/Smallcap) too, exactly as today's session showed.

Strategy Corner
Today's Concept
Dollar-Cost Averaging (SIP)

Dollar-Cost Averaging — known in India as a SIP (Systematic Investment Plan) — means investing a fixed amount at regular intervals, regardless of price, rather than trying to find the "perfect entry point." It removes the emotional guesswork of "is now the right time to buy" entirely. Today's session is a textbook case for this concept: the Nifty now looks "extended" to analysts (Dixena), and anyone attempting a lump-sum entry right now would be caught in exactly the kind of timing-guesswork that a SIP avoids. For a disciplined SIP investor, today's 2%+ move is neither a reason for FOMO nor for panic — they simply continue their regular investment, independent of the market's mood. A "concept" tag, sequential pick — and a particularly relevant reminder given today's narrow, extended-looking rally. This is purely an educational concept, not a recommendation for any specific trade or fund.
