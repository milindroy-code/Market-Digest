"The four most expensive words in the English language are 'this time it's different.'"

— Sir John Templeton

At 10 AM this morning, the outcome the whole week had been building toward finally landed — and it was a genuine surprise. Rather than the 25bp hike (to 5.50%) that 62% of economists (38 of 61) in a Reuters poll had expected, the RBI's MPC held the repo rate unchanged at 5.25% — now the fifth consecutive meeting without a move — and retained its "neutral" stance. Governor Sanjay Malhotra's post-policy press conference began at noon and is still underway as of writing; his exact remarks, along with the market's confirmed intraday reaction (the precise point-level move in the Nifty/Sensex), aren't independently confirmed in this run — that will be covered in tonight's evening wrap alongside the final closing levels. This is almost exactly the scenario today's own morning-edition explainer described: a well-expected event (here, a hike) not happening can still move markets sharply, because the real uncertainty sits in the stance and the guidance language, not the rate number itself. Historically, when the RBI delivers a more dovish surprise than expected (a hold instead of a hike), rate-sensitive sectors — banking, auto, realty — are typically the fastest and sharpest to react (in a similar hold-surprise in April 2026, these sectors jumped as much as 5-7% within hours) — which is why Bank Nifty is the one to watch most closely today, to see whether yesterday's late-session reversal (+1.11%, to 55,054.35) holds up after this decision. No circuit filter has been seen as of writing — today's single biggest, clearest breaking item is the RBI's surprise hold itself.

Strategy Corner
Today's Concept
Long Strangle

Like yesterday's Long Straddle, but you buy a call and a put at different (out-of-the-money) strikes instead of the same one. It's cheaper to put on than a straddle because both legs are further from the money, but the underlying needs to move even further before you profit. A day like today — where the outcome ran opposite to what was expected — is exactly the kind of setup where traders reach for a directionless, big-move bet like this one. This is an educational concept, not a recommendation to place the trade.
