Explainer: Pyramiding vs. Averaging Down

When a stock like Tech Mahindra jumps 7.75% in a single day, an old trading dilemma resurfaces: add more, or lock in the gains already made? The answer hinges on whether you're "averaging down" or "pyramiding." Averaging down means buying more of a losing position to lower your average cost — risky, since you're adding to a trade that's already going against you. Pyramiding is the opposite: adding to a winning position as it moves in your favor. Most experienced traders favor pyramiding into strength over averaging into weakness, since it means the market is confirming the thesis rather than you fighting it. Today's IT rally is exactly the kind of setup where this distinction matters.
